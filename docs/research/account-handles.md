# Account handle namespaces

Research checked on 2026-10-27. This report is evidence and a proposed policy for adoption by the account design owner; it does not change the account contract or implementation.

## Conclusion and scope

Use one case-insensitive namespace for people and organizations, backed by immutable account IDs. Keep public handles simple and display names independent. Maintain a small, reviewed reservation registry and an evidence-based impersonation process. Separate three decisions that products often combine: whether an old name resolves, whether it can be assigned again, and whether the old account still exists.

There is **no shared industry expiry period**. GitHub releases a renamed username immediately but holds a deleted one for 90 days; GitLab redirects until reclamation; Reddit and Mastodon retain deleted usernames indefinitely. See [GitHub deletion][gh-deletion], [GitLab redirects][gl-redirect], [Reddit][rd-rename] and [Mastodon][ma-moving]. The recommendation here is a 30-day rename redirect followed by permanent non-reassignment, and permanent non-reassignment after deletion without a redirect to a live profile. This favors continuity and resistance to impersonation over recycling desirable names. It is a product recommendation, not a consensus requirement.

GitHub users and organizations and GitLab users and top-level groups are close namespace analogues. Other comparisons have limits: AT Protocol names are DNS names across providers; Mastodon and Lemmy names are instance-scoped; Reddit communities, Discord servers and Lemmy communities have separate naming systems. Discourse users and groups compete for mention names but have different public URL prefixes. None of those distinctions justifies giving a REZICS organization a second claim to an already-owned `/@handle`.

Only primary policy documents, specifications and project source were used. Source repositories were shallow-cloned and inspected as untrusted text; nothing from them was installed, built or executed. Temporary clones were kept in `.temp/account-handles-refs/` inside this worktree: the explicit worktree-only write restriction and repository temporary-file rule took precedence over the suggested external clone directory. Source snapshots, paths and licences are recorded below. Absence of a rule in inspected documentation is reported as unspecified, not as permission or proof that no internal rule exists.

The common pattern is a distinct, constrained lookup identifier and a more expressive display name, with case variants prevented from becoming different owners. There is no common minimum or maximum: GitLab's 255 and DNS's 253 serve very different URL systems from Discord's 32 or X's 15. Restricting REZICS to ASCII avoids Unicode confusable and normalization policy in the handle; it does not prevent misleading ASCII names or impersonation through display names.

For REZICS, maintain one reviewed registry with canonical name, reason, scope and review notes. Derive actual route collisions from REZICS routing; add a short explicit list for official staff/support/security/brand identities. Check the same registry on human registration, organization creation and rename, including administrative operations unless a specifically authorized assignment is being made. Keep dynamic dispute holds separate from static routing reservations. Record changes and test them against routes when a route is added.

Do not automatically reserve every catalogue title or every well-known person's name. Ordinary names have legitimate shared uses, and the catalogue can contain fictional characters, products and works. A small preventive list can protect unusually high-risk identities; larger claims need evidence and review. The Bluesky famous-account list demonstrates that pre-reservation is possible, not that popularity creates universal ownership rights.

The inspected GitLab source also records vacated/deleted **project** paths in `Authn::BurnedProjectRoute`, keyed by organization and original project ID. This is an additional project-path protection, not a permanent user-handle reservation; the documented namespace redirect/reclamation rule should not be read as unrestricted reuse of every project URL. See [the model][gl-burned] and `app/models/route.rb`.

These policies form several families, not one majority rule that can dictate REZICS retention:

- Immediate reuse with weak or no profile continuity: GitHub and ordinary X rename.
- Conditional forwarding until takeover: GitLab namespace paths.
- Stable account identity beneath a mutable handle: AT Protocol's DID; within other products, account IDs and retained relationships serve a similar purpose.
- Strong non-reassignment: Reddit and Mastodon deletion, and the inspected Lemmy tombstone path.
- Operator-defined or undocumented release details: Discord and instance-administered software.

Redirecting a URL while allowing a new owner to override it creates a deliberate takeover boundary. Permanently forwarding every old name can also expose an unwanted identity connection. A product needs separate rules for continuity, privacy and allocation; a single expiring “reserved” state cannot express all three.

The shared policy pattern is contextual deception review with operator enforcement, rather than automatically awarding every matching string to the most famous claimant. Trademark infringement, impersonation, inactivity and technical reservation are distinct grounds. GitHub and GitLab expressly differ on whether a trademark claim can produce an allocation remedy; that difference should be an explicit REZICS choice.

A catalogue entry is not an account, and its label is not proof of representation. A person may legitimately share a name with a known person; an organization may share a word with a work or product. “Official”, a matching avatar, false affiliation, linked-domain evidence and behavior provide context. Verified representation should attach the immutable account ID to the catalogue subject through governance review, not arise from owning its title as a handle.

## Proposed policy for the account design owner

1. **Canonical form:** one claim table across people and organizations, keyed by an immutable account ID and ASCII-lowercase handle. For new assignments, use 3–32 characters, ASCII letters/digits with internal `_` or `-`, and alphanumeric ends. Case-fold before validation; reject whitespace rather than silently trimming or transliterating. Repeated internal separators may remain allowed. Keep expressive Unicode display names independent. These exact bounds and punctuation choices are local recommendations; grandfather existing valid claims if limits tighten.
2. **Reservations:** use separate system-reserved, dispute-held and historical-name states. Maintain a small version-controlled system registry with reasons, route coverage checks and an audited administrator exception for assigning an official identity. Enforce it for every account kind and rename path. Do not reserve all catalogue labels, brands by substring, or generic dictionary words. Monitor abuse through reports as well as preventive rules.
3. **Rename:** change the active handle atomically. Keep old-name forwarding for 30 days, then stop forwarding while retaining a permanent non-reassignment record. Resolve directly to the account's current handle, avoiding alias chains. Use a temporary HTTP redirect with a bounded cache lifetime, because deletion or an approved privacy unlink can end forwarding early. Bind mentions, relationships and authorization to account IDs. Limit rename frequency and audit changes to prevent inexpensive exhaustion of permanently retired names.
4. **Historical-name ownership:** allow the same live account to recover its own historical name after the same validation/governance checks; never let ordinary registration assign it to a different account. Apply retirement prospectively: do not seize a name that another account already legitimately reclaimed under the old policy. Removal of a public identity link should end public forwarding, without releasing the name to an impersonator. Permanent non-reassignment retains the minimum allocation record, not the old public profile. State this retention in the account policy; the privacy presentation and allocation record are different concerns.
5. **Deletion:** on deletion fencing, stop live-profile forwarding from every alias; retain allocation tombstones for the current and historical names indefinitely. A request for `/@deleted` should get a neutral missing/gone result, not discover a new owner or an operator identity. The public profile API may continue returning an empty result. Do not treat expiry of cleanup work or a recovery window as release authorization. If future namespace scarcity requires recycling, change the policy explicitly rather than implicitly expiring a redirect row.
6. **Disputes:** provide a report for impersonation, fraudulent affiliation, staff impersonation and rights complaints. Collect the claimed identity, affected handle, misleading context and control evidence such as a verified domain or authorized organizational contact. Review catalogue subject linkage through existing governance. A title match, fame, an inactive account or an unverified trademark assertion alone does not win the name. Allow clearly labeled commentary, fan and parody accounts when they do not deceive.
7. **Remedies and appeal:** temporarily freeze contested allocation when necessary; notify the holder with a reasonable response period except where immediate abuse containment is needed; record reasons and offer appeal. Prefer clarification or forced rename for deception. Allow exceptional reassignment only after documented governance review of authority and competing legitimate uses; assigning a spelling must never transfer the old account ID, content, membership or permissions. Retirement is the default when entitlement remains ambiguous. Legal demands need their own handling, rather than an automatic name-matching rule.

If adopted, the owning document should specify the new length rule, registry ownership, forwarding versus retirement states, same-account recovery, deletion presentation and complaint authority. Subsequent implementation acceptance should cover cross-kind/case collisions, reserved names on every claim path, exact 30-day boundary, denial of third-party historical claims after expiry, repeated renames without chains, deletion of all aliases, privacy unlinking, verification independence and reviewed exceptions. This research does not alter those acceptance tests or runtime behavior.
