# Canonical ordered coverage of the public subject index

Research evidence, 2026-10-28. This report recommends a Search design for later adoption; it does not change the owning contract or claim native implementation. The concurrent independent report was not consulted.

## Finding and recommendation

A ready Semantic identity basis proves maintained mappings, not the contents of an OpenSearch generation. The smallest reusable production proof is a **persisted certificate for each physical index incarnation and source home**, obtained from a captured structural tuple plus a finite source-position barrier, after that generation's real consumer has covered the barrier. Reuse the certificate while the structural tuple stays equal, irrespective of later ordinary positions. Position is only a catch-up witness. It must never enter the identity digest.

This works only with a certified initial population, complete anchor invalidation delivery, and protection against older in-flight projection writes. Those conditions are not all provided by the inspected code. In particular, inherited repoint emits no event the subject indexer accepts; existing reconciliation compares counts rather than identities; conflict-only bulk success does not independently establish refresh visibility; and manually populated native test generations have no consumer provenance. A ready basis plus today's checkpoint is therefore insufficient. Adding a positive readiness callback would conceal these gaps.

Recommend the bounded per-home certificate variant for steady state, with a real new-incarnation replay or paged reconciliation for uncertified old indexes. Implement the writer/delivery and visibility conditions first. Keep the existing actor-neutral sealed mapping and all approved native signatures unchanged. No new global or actor clock, holder directory, Boolean interpreter, cross-home transaction or request-time full-index traversal is needed. Bootstrap is asynchronous finite work, not a constant-cost prepare operation.

A pure repoint of an already-redirected, already-deleted original need not change index membership. ## Proof obligation and smallest barrier protocol

Let `T[h] = (partition, epoch, home, structuralGeneration)` and `B = digest(T[0..4095])`. A certificate `C[indexUUID,h]` states that an established population of this exact physical index has applied all relevant structural anchor invalidations through a captured position `P[h]` for `T[h]`, with effects search-visible and no older writer able to undo them. It is not an assertion that every present document is currently visible or every ordinary edit has been indexed.

A sufficient protocol, conditional on the writer invariants below, is:

1. Pin alias resolution, concrete index name, numeric generation and actual OpenSearch index UUID. Require one active target and known producer/population provenance. A scope with no hits performs these same observations. No hit may select the generation.
2. Capture a first fixed-home sweep. For each home, open a short RR read-only transaction, call existing `readIdentityHome(client, h)`, and read `core.partition.position` on **that same client and snapshot**. Check native availability and zero pending; release before another home. Keep tuple plus `P[h]`. No cross-home SQL join is needed.
3. Perform a second independently released complete native sweep, compare structural tuples and readiness, and compute the specified versioned digest. Positions may differ: ordinary writes are allowed. Use the first sweep's barrier, which includes every structural mutation visible in that first ready observation. Do not demand equal positions or chase a moving ordinary head. The position query is internal Search catch-up evidence; it need not change any native owner signature.
4. For a home whose persisted certificate already matches index UUID, producer version and `T[h]`, retain it. For other homes require same-incarnation consumer coverage through `P[h]`, including the correct epoch/migration lineage. Missing checkpoints cannot be treated as completed nonzero work. If the home has moved, re-establish lineage or replay under the new owner; a numerically large old checkpoint is insufficient.
5. Establish search visibility of all covered effects, including superseding writes responsible for accepted conflicts. Drain/fence older external applies. With today's conflict acceptance, a checked explicit refresh of the physical index after checkpoint observations is a necessary extra visibility step unless the writer path is repaired to provide equivalent evidence. Reject shard failures. Neither refresh nor a checkpoint establishes missing-event delivery.
6. Revalidate the full native basis independently and verify alias/index UUID before publication. Store the certificate by exact tuple/incarnation with a conditional update that cannot overwrite a newer or retired certificate. Concurrent certifiers may duplicate work; they cannot certify another tuple by copying a newer checkpoint number. A crash before certificate commit causes repetition; a crash after commit leaves reusable evidence.
7. Before exposing a raw ordered window, and on continuation/final handoff, independently verify basis/readiness and physical identity again. Bracket engine execution as needed to reject a structural mutation between validation and fetch. Different structural basis or alias/incarnation requires `search_restart_required`; unavailable/pending evidence is explicit readiness. Later ordinary head movement does not invalidate `B` or a matching certificate.

Monotonic generations, truthful pending, and complete equal sweeps provide a stable observation interval, not a global transaction. A structural write after the last observation remains subject to current/final revalidation; no scheme here promises an atomic snapshot across PostgreSQL and OpenSearch or protection against future writes. An ABA mapping change still changes generation. Epoch/home equality must also be checked; a physical index UUID prevents name-reuse ABA.

Inductive justification: start with known empty or actually reconciled population. Each covered structural event rereads its exact anchor at a source position at least as new as that committed event and deletes redirects or restores live roots. Complete delivery plus ordered/fenced effects eliminates stale structural IDs through `P[h]`. Equality of ready structural tuples at final observation rules out unnoticed intervening structural changes. Subsequent ordinary writes use native current source snapshots and cannot create an ID that was already redirected at that snapshot; they may leave visibility/filter fields stale. A later structural write invalidates this induction until its certificate is renewed.

### Limits of existing apply/checkpoint behavior

The intended refresh-visible-checkpoint rule is stronger than what the inspected duplicate-apply path proves. `projectBatch` releases its checkpoint lock before I/O, so two processes may apply the same batch. Process X writes a new version and waits for refresh. Process Y sees 409 for that same version, accepts it, and can CAS the checkpoint before X's write is visible. OpenSearch's bulk/write source explicitly skips refresh waiting when there is no translog location. A refresh after checkpoint observation closes this visibility race; accepting 409 alone does not. A deleted-missing 404 must also be interpreted from the actual delete operation response, not used as general completion evidence.

There is a second, longer-lived race: an old snapshot with a live original can remain in flight while a newer delete is applied and certified. After external delete-version retention expires, that delayed lower-version index request can recreate the document. `VersionType.EXTERNAL` rejects lower versions only while a current version exists; `InternalEngine` prunes deletion entries. Periodic refresh does not prevent this resurrection. The runner's per-process `inFlight` set is not cross-process fencing.

A certificate producer must therefore serialize/drain writes per index incarnation and home, across helper and worker writers, and fail certification if an older request has an unresolved outcome. Do not release this condition merely because an HTTP timeout elapsed. One conservative recovery is to leave that incarnation uncertified and rebuild to a fresh, never-reused physical index name and UUID after fencing its writers; late requests still target the old name. An alternative durable deletion fence requires its own concrete design. This is an actual writer gap to prove, not a claim that a finite tombstone retention setting provides a permanent guarantee.

| Per-home derived coverage                                                     | Incremental reuse for unchanged homes; only changed homes need new finite barriers              | At most `4096 × liveGenerations` current rows per prefix, plus bounded generation control/frontiers | Needs Search-owned durable metadata and its real producer; not delivered by Semantic's generation/pending table. Replay needs complete retained events or a source snapshot plus replay cut. Counts alone and “new index exists” are insufficient; ordinary hide catch-up may remove B.         |

A global digest certificate is the smallest record if global quiescence is acceptable. The per-home vector is the smallest option here that avoids requiring unrelated ordinary catch-up whenever a structural home changes. It still cannot guarantee completion under perpetual structural changes or pending maintenance. No option recovers an event that was never emitted merely by advancing a watermark.

Bootstrap must be explicit. For old generations, pending-zero native metadata must first represent all existing mappings, and Search must separately establish its initial population. Replay from a genuinely empty new index using a fresh consumer can do this when the retained log is complete and all actual current-state anchor changes have invalidations. Pruned logs, direct SQL seeds and historical missing events require bounded source/index reconciliation, not resetting checkpoints past gaps. Incrementally page both current source anchors and indexed IDs to detect missing **and extra** IDs, using native sealed original mapping and a full ready basis, then revalidate before sealing. This maintenance may inspect the public index's actual raw keys; it must not build a second holder/query engine or rewrite IDs in fetched scope results. Delete an obsolete original and populate the terminal from its own anchor source; never copy an alias's payload under a new ID.

Reconciliation pages have fixed limits and durable keyset frontiers, with total work proportional to the population. They are not an unbounded request scan. Structural changes during a page invalidate its coverage obligation; later completion must prove the current tuple. Ordinary source changes can remain eventually consistent. Cardinality agreement remains only an auxiliary diagnostic.

### Native helper and default-worker composition gap

The frozen raw-prefix fixture writes an index directly, never runs `createSubjectIndexer` and creates no `subjectConsumer` checkpoints or coverage metadata. It hides B **before** prepare, and also tests a manually created empty generation. Running the production catch-up worker during that prepare would delete B and break the expected raw prefix. Claiming “a real source wrote these documents” does not tell a later reader which structural basis governed those writes.

Thus checkpoint-only certification cannot satisfy those positive cases as currently composed. Their producer path needs real native provenance: known-empty physical incarnation establishment, structural observations associated with source snapshots, acknowledged refresh-visible writes, and persisted per-home coverage under the same fencing protocol. An empty generation needs evidence of actual emptiness and controlled writers, not an assumed zero-hit response. The existing `createSubjectIndex(search, prefix, generation)`, `SubjectSnapshot` and `bulkLines` interfaces carry no such complete handoff; `createSubjectIndex` has no database dependency. The report recommends a Search-owned native population/projection coordinator reused by helper and default worker, with data-bearing source observations, rather than a caller-supplied `canonicalReady` flag. Its exact wiring through existing helper calls is required implementation work; **this report does not claim those unmodified call sites already publish certificates**.

If integration cannot establish that native producer provenance, the honest outcome for an unknown generation is `index_not_ready` until an actual bounded audit/replay completes. That is a diagnostic limit, not a proposed relaxation of the frozen positive tests. A manager adopting the design must require proof of the helper route as well as the worker route; changing the test to seed a certificate or drain B is not acceptable. Avoid a prepare-time full scan, hidden fact-driver work, or a special fixture-prefix exception.

The default production path is `src/search/work.ts:searchWorkModule` → `startSearchIndexing` → `createSubjectIndexer`, with actual `subjectSource` and `subjectListingSource`. `scripts/search.ts:rebuild` relies on worker discovery rather than locally indexing. Certificates must be produced there durably and survive process restart. An HTTP `createSearchScopeSource` must only read verified evidence and run the shared compiler/evaluator; it cannot depend on someone having supplied an optional ready stub. Worker and helper paths must share the real coverage coordinator and incarnation lifecycle.

## Fixed-home and physical work budget

Let `H=4096`, `G` be controlled live generations (`G≤3` under managed rebuild), `N` subjects/documents, `E` replayed envelopes, `b` a bounded maintenance page, and `q` raw candidate allowance.

- **One complete basis observation:** two released sweeps = **8192 metadata rows plus core-home observations**, even with zero hits. If each home port reads one metadata and one core row, that is 16384 point-row observations, plus empty/unavailable seeks and transaction-control statements. Exact SQL statement batching is owned by the unfinished native implementation; do not call this one free digest read.
- **Cold barrier capture:** capture positions in the first sweep's same RR clients: up to 4096 additional core-position probes if they cannot reuse the placement observation; do not compare those positions in the second sweep. Read up to 4096 checkpoint points and publish up to 4096 home certificates per index. Returning only a digest from `readIdentityBasis` cannot substitute for the captured vector: use `readIdentityHome` and the specified digest encoding, sharing the native encoding utility to prevent drift.
- **Independent final revalidation:** another full basis observation costs another 8192 metadata rows plus core observations. Cold capture followed by final validation therefore costs at least 16384 metadata observations, separately from coverage/checkpoint reads. Prepare, final handoff and every required continuation observation must each be reserved and charged; no per-candidate digest expansion. A warm persisted certificate avoids catch-up/rebuild work, not native basis revalidation.
- **Metadata bound:** at most `H×G` current coverage rows per prefix (12288 at three generations), plus O(G) control and bounded frontiers. Basis vectors/digests have O(H) transient work. Semantic's per-original obligations belong to the approved native mapping authority and are not a new Search subject directory.
- **Catch-up:** O(E) envelopes and actual anchor payload work in separate batches; `BATCH_ENVELOPES=64`, source snapshots cap 256 IDs. An envelope's event fanout and source payload/history cost still matter. Preserve existing source limits; a constant batch or few returned hits is not a constant physical-I/O proof. Refresh cost depends on the physical shards/writes.
- **Bootstrap/reconciliation:** O(N+E) total work with bounded `b` pages and durable advancing frontiers. Grow old redirect/history and extra-index populations independently. This cost must not be hidden inside prepare, a native metadata point, or a readiness predicate.
- **Raw prefix:** at most reserved `q` returned/reread/rejected/lookahead/driver keys, with no refill or after-fetch relabeling. Engine postings, nested facts, sort, response bytes, index shards and native evidence are distinct costs. A constant number of returned hits proves no latency, postings or SQL bound. Index UUID/alias checks and refresh/verification requests are separately charged infrastructure work.

## Native acceptance needed for adoption

The report-only acceptance checks report existence; no runtime code, acceptance tests or gates were changed, and no runtime checks were run. The implementation should retain every existing frozen prefix assertion and add native evidence for these distinct proof obligations:

- Index A/B/C through the actual native producer, certify its physical incarnation at a real basis, hide B and append unrelated ordinary writes while indexing is paused. Prepare/prefix still return the genuine ordered raw B, charged lookahead and real matched names; current disclosure rejects B. No prepare-time raw search or worker drain is allowed.
- Merge a stored original to a lexically larger survivor; pause the consumer after native identity sealing. Native ready alone cannot prepare canonical coverage. After accepted source-home anchor events become refresh-visible, certify and observe only canonical stored IDs. Exercise inherited repoint and verification reversal/unmerge using real writers; inspect their actual envelope type, subject and route.
- Interleave capture, mutation, pending registration, stale ack, consume, certificate publication and final revalidation. A zero-pending observation before a later mutation cannot certify the newer basis. Concurrent ordinary positions must not force restart or require another global zero. Epoch/home movement must invalidate in-flight coverage while preserving only legitimately transferred seals.
- Create real zero-hit and manually populated generations through the helper route; prove native population provenance without fixture certificates, callbacks or fake hits. Verify default work registration produces the same evidence and restart resumes it. An uncertified old population with a stale extra ID cannot be certified by equal counts or a current checkpoint.
- Force a duplicate external-version conflict before the winning write refreshes; hold the refresh and ensure no certificate is published. Fail a bulk item, partial refresh, timeout and partial search shard independently. Neither error nor unproven coverage can become empty/exhausted.
- Hold an older live-document snapshot across structural deletion/certification and beyond deletion-version retention, including an ambiguous I/O timeout. Prove the writer fence prevents resurrection, or that the affected incarnation remains uncertified and is replaced. A per-process mutex is insufficient evidence.
- Switch alias, retire and recreate the same generation name, and race late publication with retirement. Verify exact UUID/protocol binding, checkpoint lineage and cleanup of coverage rows. Replay with a log gap must stop; bootstrap missing/extra IDs must use bounded advancing pages.
- Measure two complete native sweeps, core points, checkpoint/coverage probes, transaction statements, empty seeks, payload blocks, bytes and requests independently. Hold page size and live generations fixed while growing subjects, redirect history, hidden/rejected rows and unrelated ordinary backlog. Assert the derived bounds rather than only result cardinality.

This is documentation-only evidence, not copied implementation or a recommendation to add Kafka. No documentation text is reproduced; Debezium's implementation is Apache-2.0, but this report relies on the official page without assuming a separate page-content licence grant.

The sources share three useful patterns: establish an initial population cut, associate progress with the real ordered producer/consumer identity, and acknowledge only after the required receiver effect. They do not supply canonical identity semantics, event completeness or a licence to replace raw ordered IDs after fetching.
