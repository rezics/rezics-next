# Account-local shelf maintenance

## Finding and recommendation

Use an account-local durable maintenance row as a transaction-scoped reader/writer gate, a persistent quiescence state, and an atomic pointer to a complete counter generation. Drain existing writers when entering maintenance; reject or retry new writers for that account until cutover. Build the replacement in bounded, checkpointed transactions, then swap one pointer and reopen writers in one transaction. Purge takes precedence over every rebuild and leaves a terminal fence. This is an inference for REZICS from the sources below, not a claim that any one source implements this complete protocol.

The cost is temporary unavailability of writes for the affected account, potentially including a long exact-type reservation. Other accounts remain available. Old complete counter reads can continue while a rebuild runs, provided every mutation that could change their meaning is fenced; initial backfill with no complete generation must instead make these reads unavailable until publication. A stale but complete old generation is not a substitute for correct live results if an unfenced writer is allowed through.

## Evidence and limits

- PostgreSQL's [row-lock conflict rules](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/explicit-locking.html#LOCKING-ROWS) say `FOR SHARE` conflicts with updates and `FOR UPDATE`, but shared holders coexist; locks end with the transaction. `FOR KEY SHARE` is insufficient against a non-key update. [Read Committed](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/transaction-iso.html) locking commands wait for a concurrent updater and then operate on its updated row. The [application consistency guidance](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/applevel-consistency.html) cautions about snapshot timing with explicit locking. These are building blocks, not a durable multi-transaction fence by themselves.
- PostgreSQL itself implements concurrent reindexing in [`src/backend/commands/indexcmds.c`](https://github.com/postgres/postgres/blob/700b422af7e26cf310caf3fb76d78f4c8ca39c1f/src/backend/commands/indexcmds.c): create replacements, wait out transactions with old index lists, build, catch up and validate, swap validity/names in a transaction, wait out old users, then retire old indexes. This is a mature PostgreSQL implementation of concurrent publication, not an account-local, page-resumable application rebuild. REINDEX uses database-specific index machinery and cannot simply be transplanted as an aggregate counter algorithm. Snapshot lifetime matters both before publication and before cleanup. Source: cached `.reference/library-shelf-maintenance/postgres`, commit `700b422af7e26cf310caf3fb76d78f4c8ca39c1f`, PostgreSQL License, `COPYRIGHT`.
- Discourse is a PostgreSQL-backed implementation of versioned, incremental reindexing. [`app/services/search_indexer.rb`](https://github.com/discourse/discourse/blob/589325e2b8466178ee83acac9ec411de555a0093/app/services/search_indexer.rb) marks search rows with an old version for later reindexing. [`app/jobs/scheduled/reindex_search.rb`](https://github.com/discourse/discourse/blob/589325e2b8466178ee83acac9ec411de555a0093/app/jobs/scheduled/reindex_search.rb) repeatedly selects limited, ordered batches of missing/outdated rows and reindexes existing records; its cleanup also includes set-based deletes. This is per-record convergence, not an atomic complete-generation aggregate and not proof of bounded cleanup. [`app/services/user_destroyer.rb`](https://github.com/discourse/discourse/blob/589325e2b8466178ee83acac9ec411de555a0093/app/services/user_destroyer.rb) invokes explicit deletion callbacks and performs substantial work in an optional transaction; [`app/jobs/regular/delete_user_posts.rb`](https://github.com/discourse/discourse/blob/589325e2b8466178ee83acac9ec411de555a0093/app/jobs/regular/delete_user_posts.rb) loops over deletion batches. Neither establishes the durable stale-writer fence required here. Source: cached `.reference/partitioned-sitemap-feeds/discourse`, commit `589325e2b8466178ee83acac9ec411de555a0093`, GPL-2.0-or-later, `LICENSE.txt`.
- GitLab's [reindex documentation](https://docs.gitlab.com/integration/advanced_search/elasticsearch/) describes a reindex and alias-based publication; its [development guidance](https://docs.gitlab.com/development/advanced_search/) describes the separate multiple-index/dual-update design as still under development. This is useful comparison, not evidence that that proposed dual-update design is already implemented or appropriate for PostgreSQL counters.

The shared lesson is to distinguish preparation from publication, make work discoverable after interruption, and account for old users and concurrent mutations. Discourse's per-row repair illustrates why a bounded page alone does not establish aggregate completeness; PostgreSQL's concurrent index phases illustrate why a completed build and a visibility switch need coordination.

## Existing REZICS authority and integration

The local [`shelf-contract.md`](../../services/kernel/src/library/shelf-contract.md) and the read-only refinement at `services/kernel/src/library/shelf-contract.md` in the specified refinement worktree require account quiescence, bounded idempotent batches, an atomic generation swap and no partial generation. The refinement specifies exact private dictionary values, roots, nodes and reservations: a collision may require multiple transactions of at most 100 edges; staged nodes are shared, and a terminal and full mapping publish atomically. Values are not workflow payloads. A stale reservation must revalidate both its account fence and source binding. The refined tables are proposed by that contract, not assumed to be present in this checkout. [`library-type-keys.md`](library-type-keys.md) supplies the identity tradeoffs; [`library-query-bulk.md`](library-query-bulk.md) recommends bounded workflow segments with native checkpoint and enqueue intent, rather than unbounded workflow history.

Account deletion is terminal under [`access/README.md`](../../services/kernel/src/access/README.md#account-deletion-policy). [`lifecycle.ts`](../../services/kernel/src/access/lifecycle.ts) locks the account and commits its tombstone/fence and, when supplied a continuation binding, the deletion continuation in the same command. [`committed-continuations.ts`](../../services/kernel/src/access/committed-continuations.ts) constructs a closed deletion allowlist; [`committed-deletion-work.ts`](../../services/kernel/src/access/committed-deletion-work.ts) explicitly owns only operator and handle steps, while [`services/auth/src/workflows.ts`](../../services/auth/src/workflows.ts) owns Auth cleanup. There is currently no Library purge step in this allowlist or runner, and a tombstone without a continuation does not create that root. Neither content erasure nor Auth consent erasure is an account-wide Library purge hook.

An actual adjacent model is [`organization-close-work.ts`](../../services/kernel/src/access/organization-close-work.ts): the organization tombstone stores a checkpoint, an account-tombstoned consumer starts deterministic page workflows, and page effects/receipts and cursor advancement are durable. It is organization-specific; its existence does not cover all account deletion paths. [`relation-facets-design.md`](../../services/kernel/src/semantic/relation-facets-design.md) describes native requested/active generations and enqueue-before-checkpoint, but its hints are a different, asynchronous consistency contract, not an existing shelf barrier.

Integration must therefore be built with the Access/account-erasure owner: ensure every authoritative account tombstone path durably establishes Library purge intent, register and compose a Library-owned bounded runner, and make whichever operation claims account purge completion await its terminal receipt. Extending the existing committed deletion allowlist requires its creation, validation, dispatcher, Auth workflow and expiry/recovery paths to agree; simply adding a function to Library or subscribing to a best-effort event does not establish that guarantee. Cover the no-continuation path and organization close explicitly. Preserve existing deletion authority and renewal rules; do not give ordinary callers a new purge power.

## Proposed protocol

**Minimum durable state.** One row keyed by `(route, account_id)` holds `mode` (`open`, `rebuilding`, `purging`, `purged`), a monotonically advanced fence epoch, active complete counter generation, and the current operation identity. A native operation/checkpoint row stores a never-reused operation/build ID, phase, keyset cursor, sequence, and durable continuation intent; it can be folded into the account row if only one operation is ever active. `shelf_count` keys include generation, bucket and compact type identity. Index all page and cleanup seeks with route/account/generation first and a stable unique key last. Store bounded keys and phase in workflow arguments, never full type values. The terminal fence or equivalent authoritative account tombstone must survive physical cleanup and prevent account-ID ABA; a missing gate must never mean an erased account is writable.

**Lock order and drain.** Define a single order across all paths: existing account/partition authority locks first, then shelf gate, then entry/session/collection, dictionary and counter rows in deterministic key order. Integrate this with actual existing command lock order before implementation; do not invert it on a delayed continuation. Every normal mutation that affects the rebuild's inputs or outputs obtains `SELECT ... FOR SHARE` on the existing gate in its _same transaction_, checks active account, `open` and the expected epoch after acquiring it, and holds the lock through commit or rollback. This includes entry create/state/delete, Collection create/delete, progress head and session changes, type retyping, dictionary publication and each reservation continuation, including internal/background paths. Existing replay/receipt handling must not perform a new mutation outside the guard. A multi-transaction reservation never holds a lock between pages; each page checks the fence and source again, and the final consuming command does so again. Prevent lazy gate creation from racing deletion by creating it under the account lock or using an equally fenced account-root initialization.

The starter locks the gate `FOR UPDATE` after the account lock. This waits for all admitted shared holders to finish; blocked entrants re-read the committed state and fail/retry. It atomically advances the epoch, sets `rebuilding`, allocates the build ID, and persists a runnable checkpoint/intent. Once committed, no database lock is held across the scan; the durable mode is the barrier. Bound lock waits and return a retryable maintenance outcome rather than waiting for the whole account scan. Transactions that started earlier but had not acquired the gate are not admitted writers. Locks do not themselves protect code paths that omit the guard.

**Pages and publication.** With source writers quiescent, seek a fixed indexed page of source rows after the native cursor; include all entry states/types and active Collections required for the count, with a separate phase/cursor for each source. Compute bounded per-page contributions. In the same transaction, lock/check the gate and operation ID/epoch, apply contributions to the unpublished generation and advance cursor/sequence, or use a unique page receipt plus deterministic contribution keys; a replay must not add the same page twice. Do not advance a cursor without the page's effects. Any source-derived fields or `progress_head` backfill needed by the refined contract require their own bounded phase and guarded writes; the counter pointer alone does not publish partially updated entry/head fields. Keep affected shelf reads unavailable during an initial field backfill unless those fields also have an equivalent complete visibility scheme. No whole-account aggregate, delete, or copy occurs at cutover.

A final short transaction locks the same gate, checks the matching run/epoch, all phase completion and account still active, switches `active_generation` to the completed build and sets `open` together. Writers that resume see the new pointer and update that generation in their own transactions. Readers join the active pointer and count rows in one SQL statement or use one consistent transaction snapshot; separate Read Committed statements can otherwise straddle a swap. A read that began before swap can finish on the old complete generation. Retire old generation rows later with indexed bounded keyset deletes; PostgreSQL MVCC protects a reader's existing snapshot from physical deletion, but a multi-statement read needs a consistent snapshot. Never collect the active or current build generation. An abandoned partial generation is collected only after it is fenced from all workers.

**Crash, expiry and retries.** Native row state, cursor and page effects commit together. Replaying a committed sequence returns its receipt/current cursor; an uncommitted page is safe to retry. Commit a durable enqueue intent with the checkpoint, use deterministic operation/segment IDs and a recovery scanner for runnable intents, and bound pages per workflow segment. A crash between commit and enqueue/ack must not strand work; duplicate enqueues cannot duplicate effects. Lease timeouts only transfer worker ownership via a monotonic attempt token/CAS; they never automatically reopen writes or authorize an old worker. If the work's committed authority expires, keep the fence and fail closed; use the existing governance renewal path for deletion and explicit authorized recovery for maintenance. A deliberate abort may reopen only after invalidating the build epoch and preventing all stale pages/publications; never switch to a partial build. Alert on a stalled or expired fence.

**Purge wins.** The tombstone transaction and/or its durable same-anchor purge intent must order against the gate with the same lock discipline, increment the epoch and set `purging` before cleanup is admitted. If a rebuild page or reservation holds the gate, purge waits for that bounded transaction; if purge wins, every later worker fails its epoch/mode/account check. Rebuild cutover and purge serialize on the gate, so neither can reopen a deleted account. Purge cancellation of a rebuild is durable; an expired cleanup authorization does not undo the tombstone or reopen the namespace.

Delete in bounded indexed pages across all generations and all relevant private rows: reservations (including full input), nodes, roots, values, counts, progress heads and applicable source metadata; respect actual foreign keys and shared node dependencies, children before parents, with cursors/receipts per phase. Other account data covered by the owning deletion policy needs its own explicit owner and completion evidence. Do not assume a cascading delete is bounded. The final receipt follows verified exhaustion of every phase under the terminal fence, and only then marks `purged`; preserve the non-resurrection authority even after cleanup. A delayed reservation has only an opaque ID and old epoch: every continuation and final publication rechecks gate, operation identity and current source binding inside its writing transaction, so it cannot recreate a value or node after purge. Cleanup and retry throughput, retention of receipts and orphaned generation storage require monitoring.

## Alternative and decisions for adoption

Dual writes could keep normal writes available, but require every writer to update old and building generations or durably capture and replay ordered deltas. Snapshot boundary, changes during scan, deletions, type identity reservations, duplicate replay and final catch-up must all be proven; direct increments into a partial scan can double count. Its higher complexity is not justified by the current contract's explicit quiescence. A transaction or advisory lock held for an entire account scan is also unsuitable: the durable state plus per-transaction guard supplies the cross-page fence instead.

Decisions for the owning contract are limited to adopting this quiescence/availability tradeoff and retry behavior; choosing the concrete page/segment limits and operational timeout/renewal policy; and naming the authoritative account-erasure completion owner and its integration for _all_ tombstone paths. Adopt the generation/epoch, checkpoint and purge precedence rules together. The exact lock order must be verified against the composed command paths, and the adopted implementation must test drain, stale reservation and purge races, page/ack crashes, old-reader snapshots and bounded cleanup before enabling cutover.
