## Recommendation to adopt

Keep the direct-sale model: REZICS captures an offer and its price, uses one direct Stripe account and hosted Checkout, owns buyer entitlements, and accounts for creator royalties paid outside Stripe. **Connect is not technically necessary for that model.** This is an architectural inference conditional on REZICS actually being the direct seller with the required distribution rights, rather than a platform collecting payments for independent sellers. Stripe's [eligibility rules](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/eligibility) and [Connect documentation](https://docs.stripe.com/connect) distinguish those arrangements. Paying royalties outside Stripe does not establish which arrangement the business has.

Preserve the current strong payment boundary: a signed event schedules a fresh, correctly associated provider read; the redirect and event body cannot grant access. Preserve the receipt-aware native revision fence, buyer-partition atomic order/entitlement change, terminal-account barrier, and separate Event/Session/Balance Transaction identities. Stripe's [fulfilment guide](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment) supports Session-based fulfilment with repeated and concurrent calls; it does not establish the repository's transaction or authorization guarantees.

Correct creation uncertainty, incomplete adverse evidence and accounting assumptions before calling the complete purchase-to-creator-settlement journey done. Treat paid purchase history, current benefit permission, provider cash movements, creator earnings, money available for payment and evidence of an external creator payment as different facts. The smallest adoption decisions are listed below; none requires a marketplace, points, a new workflow engine or live Stripe calls.

### Evidence vocabulary

- **Documented fact** means a linked current Stripe document, API reference, terms section or pinned official source says it.
- **Observed implementation** means static source behavior in this checkout. A passing-looking test assertion is evidence of intent, not a fresh runtime result.
- **Inference** connects those facts to this direct-sale model. It is conditional where Stripe account approval or business facts are unknown.
- **Recommendation** is a proposed contract correction, not an already adopted requirement or a claim about Stripe.
- **Unknown** means the sources or accessible repository do not establish the answer. Unknowns stay visible in fixtures and reconciliation rather than becoming zero, success or absence of a refund.

## Managed Payments and the business boundary

**Documented fact:** the [eligibility page](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/eligibility) requires review, a supported business location and eligible automated digital products/tax codes. It supports direct integrations, excluding Connect platforms, Express accounts and platform-controlled accounts. The seller must sell directly and have the necessary rights. A US account, a `txcd_`-shaped string and a creator royalty percentage do not prove those conditions.

The [Managed Payments terms](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms#stripe-managed-payments), modified September 28, 2026, are more precise than “Stripe sells the content.” Sections 2.1 and 3.1 make Sold through Link, LLC the merchant of record while the user remains seller of record; the user's account is treated as connected to SMP as platform. Sections 3.3–3.4 allow Stripe refunds and leave refund/dispute/reversal liability with the seller. This internal SMP relationship does **not** require REZICS to create creator connected accounts.

The [operating guide](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/how-it-works) describes Link checkout/receipts, payment support, dispute management and support escalation. Product fulfilment/support remain REZICS responsibilities. Stripe can refund without a local refund command, including after a support escalation unanswered for 48 hours; it can issue refunds within 60 days in certain cases. This is not a maximum lifetime for all legally required refunds or chargebacks, nor insurance against debits. [Tax compliance](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/tax-compliance) is jurisdiction-limited, not a promise that all seller tax obligations disappear.

**Inference:** if creators license works to REZICS and REZICS genuinely sells the resulting benefit directly, an internal royalty payable and external payment reference are compatible with this architecture. If creators are actually independent sellers using REZICS as a platform, the existing direct-sale/Managed Payments assumption needs a product and eligibility decision. Do not solve that legal/business difference by silently adding `transfer_data`, `on_behalf_of`, application fees or a creator funds API: the [Managed Payments migration guide](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/update-checkout) explicitly excludes Connect integration parameters.

**Availability limitation:** the current pages read do not label this entire Managed Payments integration as a universal public preview or guarantee access to this account. Actual review, activation, accepted terms, supported products and account configuration remain external conditions. A historical beta label, a marketing description and a skill's “marketplace” routing rule are all insufficient evidence either to approve REZICS or to omit signed-fixture behavior.

The [migration parameter tables](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/update-checkout#remove-unsupported-parameters) also matter: Managed Payments controls tax/payment methods and always enables Adaptive Pricing; its Sessions cannot combine `automatic_tax` or an `adaptive_pricing` override with that integration. The adapter's mutually exclusive Managed Payments/fallback branches are appropriate. Do not apply generic skill advice about payment-method configuration to fields this product disallows.

### Four different meanings of settlement

| Fact                         | Evidence and meaning                                                                                                                                                                | What it cannot establish                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Buyer paid                   | Correctly bound Checkout Session `status=complete`, `payment_status=paid`; see [Session object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/object).                              | Current entitlement after a refund/dispute; available cash; creator payment.                                                              |
| Stripe account cash movement | `txn_…` signed `amount`, `fee`, `net`, currency and availability; see [Balance Transaction](https://docs.stripe.com/api/balance_transactions/object).                               | A creator's contractual share or a bank receipt.                                                                                          |
| Ordinary Stripe payout       | A `po_…` moves Stripe account funds to its bank account/debit card; see [payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/payouts) and [Payout object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/payouts/object). | Distribution of royalties to REZICS creators. `paid` can subsequently become failed; arrival estimates are not external creator receipts. |
| Creator statement/payment    | REZICS accruals, adjustments and an operator's attestation of an actual outside-Stripe payment.                                                                                     | A Stripe balance, payout, Connect transfer or proof supplied by an internal row.                                                          |

Stripe's [payout reconciliation report](https://docs.stripe.com/reports/payout-reconciliation) associates transactions with automatic payouts; manual/instant payout cases do not all have that automatic allocation. A [Transfer](https://docs.stripe.com/api/transfers/object) is a separate Stripe account-to-account object. Neither report nor object makes a royalty statement a funds movement. Recommendation: keep these terms separate in contracts and UI; explain that the existing `order.settlement='paid'` means historical purchase payment, not irreversible financial finality.

## Corrected payment and evidence model

The following is the recommended contract shape for adoption, not a replacement implementation in this research change.

The native routing, authorization and atomicity rules are REZICS recommendations/constraints, not Stripe guarantees.

### Smallest concrete flow

1. **Capture intent in the buyer partition.** Authorize the verified active buyer and offer, commit immutable order terms and the native receipt/outbox. Persist creation-attempt parameters/time before contacting Stripe. Follow the existing shared command receipt rules; another account, including one sharing the route, cannot replay/disclose the result.
2. **Create hosted Checkout outside the native transaction.** Use the existing captured inline item, quantity one, mode payment, explicit adopted tax behavior, direct-account Managed Payments configuration and stable creation key. Record the returned Session binding atomically. Preserve uncertain outcomes; enforce the retention cutoff described above. [Session creation](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create) supports the necessary inline fields in the pinned types.
3. **Verify and durably admit the event.** Bound exact raw bytes and signature header, verify against the configured endpoint secrets/clock policy, then classify. Await durable Event-ID enqueue before a successful HTTP acknowledgement. Invalid signatures have no domain/durable effects; authenticated but unsupported/foreign/malformed events acknowledge without payment effects under the existing policy. A durable queue failure must remain retryable, not a successful acknowledgement.
4. **Resolve and serialize the Session.** Resolve event Session directly or locate it through PaymentIntent, validate native binding, and enter the existing keyed effects queue. Each distinct event/repair/expiry gets a distinct refresh intent. The resolver is routing only; it cannot grant buyer authority or carry a stale full read into apply.
5. **Admit, read, apply.** Keep receipt-aware native admission/revision under the order lock, commit/release, retrieve fresh associated provider evidence with physical budgets, then apply only that captured revision. A superseded attempt cannot apply old bytes. Unknown/incomplete evidence cannot turn into absence of a block. Local revision order is not provider snapshot isolation.
6. **Commit buyer effects together.** In one short buyer-partition command, preserve historical payment truth, record new named financial movements, decide the existing entitlement's grant/revoke/restore and emit native receipt/outbox. The Accounts terminal barrier overrides grants/restores, including on late payment; financial evidence may still be recorded. Never hold a buyer transaction across Stripe, DBOS-system persistence or a creator-partition command.
7. **Accrue creator effects separately and reconcile.** Relay named recorded transaction IDs into the creator-partition idempotent statement command. Track unresolved completeness/allocation explicitly. A replay adds no line or empty later-month statement. Reconcile provider gaps and externally attested payment without converting an operator assertion into a Stripe transfer.

| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `complete` + `paid`, matching captured positive-price terms, complete adverse-state evidence                           | Paid, retained through later refund/dispute.                                                                  | Grant once, or restore same entitlement only when all blocks cleared and buyer non-terminal.                                |
| `complete` + `unpaid`, processing/delayed payment                                                                      | Pending/current unpaid; no paid claim.                                                                        | No grant. Wait for fresh evidence after asynchronous success/failure or bounded reconciliation.                             |

Failure is not a proof that no later valid payment can ever occur.                                                |
| Ambiguous creation/read, association mismatch, missing required adverse-state data, unsupported status or cap exceeded | Unknown if never confirmed; preserve historical paid if already known. Record reconciliation debt separately. | Freeze last verified decision; no new grant/restore based on missing data.                                                  |
| Complete paid plus partial refund under adopted indivisible-benefit policy                                             | Paid; record only actual signed financial movements.                                                          | Retain benefit; aggregate full pending/requires-action refund provisionally revokes, succeeded full refund remains revoked. |

Stripe documents payment-status and delayed-event behavior in [fulfilment](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment); refund and dispute states in the [Refund](https://docs.stripe.com/api/refunds/object) and [Dispute](https://docs.stripe.com/api/disputes/object) references. The benefit policy, freeze-on-incomplete rule and terminal precedence above are proposed/preserved REZICS rules. `payment_status=paid` does not wait for Stripe balance availability; absence of an available balance transaction must not be confused with absence of a successful payment.

Ordering is state-based: a refund can precede the first processed completion, a duplicate completion can follow revocation, and reinstatement can follow a statement already paid. Re-read the associated current evidence and apply the native fence; do not order these by webhook arrival, Event time, request start time or redirect. Reconciliation must handle missed events as well as known pending orders and unresolved creation, without relying on a global paid-order sweep.

## Creator accounting and statement semantics

### Provider facts, allocations and reserves

Retain provider movement `amount`, `fee`, `net`, currency, exchange rate and source key without rewriting them; the documented invariant is `net = amount - fee`. Record each charge, refund, refund failure reversal, dispute withdrawal and reinstatement once. A status change without a movement is access evidence, not a synthetic financial posting. See [Balance Transaction](https://docs.stripe.com/api/balance_transactions/object), [Refund](https://docs.stripe.com/api/refunds/object) and [Dispute](https://docs.stripe.com/api/disputes/object).

Recommendation: distinguish three calculations in the contract and statement explanation:

1. **Provider attributed net:** sum actual order-associated provider net movements by settlement currency. Keep unrelated account adjustments in separate reconciliation, not arbitrarily assigned to an order.
2. **Creator earnings basis:** provider attributed net, adjusted only by explicitly evidenced remaining tax obligations and adopted platform-borne costs. If REZICS bears dispute fees, represent that as an allocation/subsidy rather than calling it provider net. Apply captured royalty basis and adopted per-currency integer rounding once at the declared aggregation boundary.
3. **Payable liability:** earned creator share plus prior same-currency carry, minus adopted reserves and prior external payments, with explicit releases. An estimated tax reserve belongs here with provenance and release conditions, not in an immutable field presented as Stripe-withheld tax.

The existing worked 1200 gross / 200 tax / 50 fee / 100 refund example yielding 850 basis and 765 at 90% is a valid **conditional fixture**, not a universal Stripe formula. Current [refund guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds) does not promise processing-fee return; [Managed Payments refund support](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/how-it-works#refund-support) warns that some refunded tax remains retained/remitted. A full refund therefore need not make account net, creator earnings or future liability exactly zero.

The report columns `withheld_tax` and `fee_net_of_withheld_tax` can support reconciliation; do not infer that every fee array contains them or that a missing entry proves zero tax. Unknown allocation means a visible accounting hold. No flat Managed Payments price, guessed FX rate, proportional “tax credit,” description-based fee classification or forced all-zero full reversal may substitute for evidence.

### Statements and outside-Stripe payment

Preserve current per-creator, per-recording-UTC-month, per-currency immutable line attribution and late adjustments. Distinguish provider creation date, REZICS recorded period and actual external payment date. A late debit against an already paid month creates a new adjustment/carry; it does not rewrite a paid statement or silently debit a creator bank account.

Recommended first policy is conservative and small: unresolved accounting evidence is not payable; negative carry remains per currency; REZICS bears the currently excluded dispute fees unless a later creator agreement changes that rule. Do not invent a reserve duration or clawback right from Stripe's dispute windows. Those are business terms requiring adoption, including what happens if a creator never earns enough future positive royalties.

`markPaid` should mean an authorized operator attests that the specifically payable amount was actually paid by the agreed external method, with reference, date, currency and amount sufficient to audit that assertion. It must reauthorize before receipt replay. A string on an open/negative/unresolved statement is not proof. Partial payments, failed external payments and correcting a mistaken attestation need either a deliberately limited initial policy or explicit adjustment states; they cannot be smuggled into Stripe payout events.

Ordinary Stripe bank settlement can fund REZICS's operations but is a separate reconciliation. The [Payout object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/payouts/object) has its own status and failure movement; [payout reconciliation](https://docs.stripe.com/reports/payout-reconciliation) is not a creator ledger. No Connect marketplace, transfer API or custody service is indispensable to the licensed direct-sale model described here.

Concrete proposed reconciliation bounds for adoption: enumerate an explicit outage interval in slices of at most 24 hours, entirely within the documented 30-day Event window; admit at most 100 Events per invocation and return a continuation instead of silently finishing a larger interval. Run known-order repair from at most 100 explicitly named routable orders per invocation, with the existing per-order provider budgets. Stop automatic uncertain creation retries at or before 24 hours from the first physical attempt, with a configured safety margin. These are proposed REZICS work limits, not provider resolution deadlines. An interval outside retention, exhausted continuation or still-unknown order remains visible operational debt; none becomes failed merely because the work budget ended.

Stripe-side list/retry facts come from [refund pagination](https://docs.stripe.com/api/refunds/list), [dispute pagination](https://docs.stripe.com/api/disputes/list), [webhook delivery](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks) and [Event recovery](https://docs.stripe.com/events/manage-webhook-endpoints). Provider retrieval is external evidence collection; native order/entitlement transactions are the authority for REZICS effects; DBOS system persistence supplies durable execution coordination. None substitutes for the other two.

Later signed-fixture/native growth axes should independently increase duplicate deliveries of one Event, distinct same-Session events, unrelated Sessions, adverse objects per payment, retained orders, native command receipts/history, DBOS history, creator statement count, lines per statement and cursor depth. Observe physical provider calls/bytes/deadlines, native rows/probes/locks/envelopes and DBOS persistence separately. Cap-bound cases must prove a visible hold and bounded exit. Existing reported base growth failures remain known work, not a new research blocker or a reason to run runtime checks here.
