# Library retention after terminal account deletion

Research date: 27 October 2026. Evidence and recommendation for adoption, not an implemented retention policy or erasure guarantee. No product code, tests or gates change in this report.

## Recommended product policy

Physically erase the account's private Library namespace. Retain only **active public Collections, including their current members and member notes, and explicitly published log snapshots** as de-identified contributions. Retain their stable account attribution, rendered through Access as “Deleted account” or “Deleted organization”. Do not grant a replacement curator, revive the account, or turn private data into a contribution.

Make these classifications explicit:

| State when authority ends at T                                                                 | Recommended disposition                                                                                          | Retention purpose and limits                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Active public Collection                                                                       | Keep its current title, description, ordered occurrences and notes; freeze further curator mutations.            | Preserve a deliberately public reading resource and existing links. Public membership pairs/counts remain accurate for these surviving Collections.                                    |
| Active unlisted Collection                                                                     | Physically erase the Collection and all occurrences/notes.                                                       | Knowing an id permits reading today, but unlisted sharing is not a promise of permanent public contribution. Do not promote it to public or retain it as a hidden archive.             |
| Private Collection, or any already-deleted Collection                                          | Physically erase the Collection and all occurrences/notes, including remnants of earlier public membership.      | Private organization and previously withdrawn material have no retained-public-resource purpose. A former public visibility is not a retention entitlement.                            |
| Collection member note                                                                         | Follow its containing Collection's disposition; retain only the current note in a surviving public Collection.   | It was published with that Collection. Do not copy notes from an erased Collection into logs, catalogue history or another Collection. Identifying text still needs a removal channel. |
| Explicitly published log still present at T                                                    | Retain only the published snapshot and deleted-account attribution; erase its source session, units and history. | Preserve the exact voluntary publication, not an updating reading diary. Never create a log from an unpublished session or restore a retracted log.                                    |
| Entries, copies, follows, all progress, shelf metadata, staged text and operational selections | Physically erase, subject only to the narrowly described replay-evidence window below.                           | These are private personal state or temporary execution data, including zero-count identities and absent-entry slots. No public counts, recommendations or “who also read” retention.  |

This is the smallest recommended policy with a clear publication boundary. Retaining public Collections avoids an invented contribution cascade; erasing unlisted Collections is a deliberate additional Library policy, not something Access's tombstone already specifies. The owner must adopt and disclose both choices before implementing effects. A public Collection later made private/unlisted, or deleted before T, follows its state at T; retain no historical public snapshot by inference. Once all writers serialize against the terminal account fence, later bounded classification observes this frozen state without a whole-account transaction at T.

Suggested confirmation language for the Library portion: “Account deletion permanently ends access. We erase your private library, copies, follows, reading progress, private and unlisted Collections. Your public Collections, their current notes, and logs you explicitly published remain under ‘Deleted account’. Remove or unpublish those contributions before deleting if you want them removed. Ask our privacy contact for a data copy or a privacy removal request, including after deletion.” Name the actual published contact and explain asynchronous cleanup and its evidence exceptions; do not promise an unimplemented completion date or self-serve export.

“De-identified” is intentional: stable ids, free text and external copies can identify someone. Product deletion does not settle statutory erasure, a retention legal basis, licence attribution obligations, identifying text removal, legal holds, or backup expiry. Access already separates privacy-contact requests from product eligibility and cleanup. A person must be able to request removal after losing login; a product-retained contribution is not an automatic exception to that request. This report proposes no new legal deadline or blanket legal hold.

## Primary-source observations

Sources were read as untrusted text; none was installed, built, run or copied into implementation. Existing main-checkout `.reference/` caches were reused, without new clones. Paths in the source register below are relative to that directory. Commit links pin actual inspected behavior; live documentation is supporting evidence, not proof of every deployed version.

### Open Library: explicit split between notes, reading-log rows and lists

At the inspected commit, [`Account.anonymize`](https://github.com/internetarchive/openlibrary/blob/37e446827465be2febad416de1bc18753b66997d/openlibrary/accounts/model.py#L455) removes group membership, replaces the profile document with `/type/delete`, deletes account/email/reset/verification/preference store entries, and deletes booknotes. It **renames**, rather than deletes, ratings, observations, bookshelf rows, merge-request submitters and bestbook entries to a generated `anonymous-…` identity. [`CommonExtras`](https://github.com/internetarchive/openlibrary/blob/37e446827465be2febad416de1bc18753b66997d/openlibrary/core/db.py#L127) confirms the actual SQL update/delete distinction. [`Bookshelves`](https://github.com/internetarchive/openlibrary/blob/37e446827465be2febad416de1bc18753b66997d/openlibrary/core/bookshelves.py#L31) uses `bookshelves_books` for reading-log shelves; renaming leaves those reading selections stored. The [internal account endpoint](https://github.com/internetarchive/openlibrary/blob/37e446827465be2febad416de1bc18753b66997d/openlibrary/fastapi/account.py#L526) invokes this method after HMAC and Internet Archive credential authentication; it is not evidence of a universal self-serve UI guarantee.

List deletion is a separate operation: [`lists_delete.process_delete`](https://github.com/internetarchive/openlibrary/blob/37e446827465be2febad416de1bc18753b66997d/openlibrary/plugins/openlibrary/lists.py#L459) invalidates list-preview/user-list caches and saves a `/type/delete` document. The [FastAPI list read/delete helpers](https://github.com/internetarchive/openlibrary/blob/37e446827465be2febad416de1bc18753b66997d/openlibrary/fastapi/lists.py#L80) reject deleted lists and reuse that operation. This is logical deletion of the current list document, not demonstrated physical history erasure. `Account.anonymize` contains no corresponding list sweep; these paths do not prove complete erasure of lists, revisions or remote search copies.

**Lesson for REZICS:** select retained categories deliberately and clean derived list discovery too. Do not adopt Open Library's renamed reading-log rows: REZICS explicitly keeps entries/progress private and prohibits that conversion into retained contributions. Its separate table transactions and account-wide SQL updates also do not supply REZICS's bounded atomic-recovery protocol.

### Discourse: retain publications through a distinct anonymizer

The official [anonymization documentation](https://meta.discourse.org/t/anonymizing-users-in-discourse/86929) distinguishes retaining contributions from user deletion and describes residual identities in revisions/logs. The pinned [`UserAnonymizer`](https://github.com/discourse/discourse/blob/589325e2b8466178ee83acac9ec411de555a0093/app/services/user_anonymizer.rb) retains the user id and posts, changes username/profile/email/password, removes authentication associations and queues further cleanup. IP replacement is optional. Its [`AnonymizeUser` job](https://github.com/discourse/discourse/blob/589325e2b8466178ee83acac9ec411de555a0093/app/jobs/regular/anonymize_user.rb) additionally clears email/invite/raw-email/custom-field data; username-history scrubbing depends on settings. The initial action can itself log old email/username when `log_anonymizer_details` is enabled. Thus a public replacement name is not a physical-data inventory or anonymity guarantee.

[`UserDestroyer`](https://github.com/discourse/discourse/blob/589325e2b8466178ee83acac9ec411de555a0093/app/services/user_destroyer.rb) refuses deletion when posts exist unless post deletion was requested. It explicitly removes bookmarks and drafts and, with `delete_posts`, invokes content deletion callbacks and `PostDestroyer`. Its optional preparation also deletes read timings, topic views and topic-user rows. These are distinct private/read-state and public-content paths; the class includes substantial account-wide work, not a proof of bounded per-account pages. Post destruction and database-row erasure must not be equated without tracing their separate implementation.

**Lesson for REZICS:** retain intentionally published content with replacement attribution while enumerating private state and asynchronous evidence separately. Do not borrow Discourse's account identity model, staff-only workflow, optional IP cleanup, or retained previous-identity logging as REZICS policy.

### BookWyrm: useful shelf comparison, with a physical-erasure gap

Official [settings documentation](https://docs.joinbookwyrm.com/settings.html#deactivating-or-deleting-your-account) describes irreversible deletion, attempts to remove user data/posts, federation limitations, and retention of independently existing books. The pinned [delete view](https://github.com/bookwyrm-social/bookwyrm/blob/fe2fa0bd2b16f1d95030d8ac1d41139e51b8cde4/bookwyrm/views/preferences/delete_user.py) calls `user.delete()` and logs out. [`User.delete`](https://github.com/bookwyrm-social/bookwyrm/blob/fe2fa0bd2b16f1d95030d8ac1d41139e51b8cde4/bookwyrm/models/user.py#L429) keeps the user row, disables reactivation, mangles email, clears profile/favorites and iterates the user's statuses. [`Status.delete`](https://github.com/bookwyrm-social/bookwyrm/blob/fe2fa0bd2b16f1d95030d8ac1d41139e51b8cde4/bookwyrm/models/status.py#L102) normally sets a deleted flag and clears content/quotation, while boosts take a different deletion path.

The inspected user method does not purge shelves, lists or readthroughs. Their models ([shelf](https://github.com/bookwyrm-social/bookwyrm/blob/fe2fa0bd2b16f1d95030d8ac1d41139e51b8cde4/bookwyrm/models/shelf.py), [list](https://github.com/bookwyrm-social/bookwyrm/blob/fe2fa0bd2b16f1d95030d8ac1d41139e51b8cde4/bookwyrm/models/list.py), [readthrough](https://github.com/bookwyrm-social/bookwyrm/blob/fe2fa0bd2b16f1d95030d8ac1d41139e51b8cde4/bookwyrm/models/readthrough.py)) use protected user relationships; a retained user row triggers no Django user-row cascade. This is an observation about these paths, not a claim that every deployment exposes those rows or that no separate cleanup exists anywhere. The cached revision's licence is Anti-Capitalist Software License 1.4, a restricted licence; this comparison is supplementary source-available evidence. Open Library and Discourse supply the two mature open-source comparisons.

**Shared pattern and limit:** all three distinguish an identity action from particular content actions; all have surviving rows or secondary cleanup worth inventorying. None establishes that private state becomes safe to retain merely by renaming an owner, or demonstrates the complete bounded purge required here. REZICS should preserve independently published resources, erase unpublished reading state, and prove both native cleanup and replay safety.

## Recommended bounded purge protocol

This is a design recommendation for this repository's PostgreSQL commands, transactional outbox and DBOS workers. It is not code to port from the reference products.

1. **Start from the committed tombstone.** Add a named Library effect for `com.rezics.access.account-tombstoned.v1` through `libraryWorkModule`/`addEffects`, composed into durable `registerEffects`. Use the terminal account/event identity as a stable native purge operation key. Its short initializer checks the authoritative tombstone, idempotently persists a native account purge row and runnable enqueue intent, and advances the maintenance epoch to `purging`. Duplicate delivery must find that same run. The event's exactly-once production does not imply exactly-once delivery/execution. Do not wait for this initializer in Access's deletion response.
2. **Make T effective even before the consumer runs.** Every ordinary Collection/personal command, bulk attempt, type reservation page/final publication, metadata projection and maintenance cutover must serialize against the authoritative terminal account check in its writing transaction. Reuse a common account/partition→maintenance gate→domain-row lock order. Drain already admitted writers with bounded lock waits; do not hold a connection across pages or waits. PostgreSQL's [row locks](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/explicit-locking.html#LOCKING-ROWS) last for a transaction; the native epoch/mode carries the fence between transactions. Missing/lazily recreated gates must never mean a deleted account is open. Old rebuilds cannot publish or reopen, old reservations cannot allocate, and stopped bulk work cannot mint new target commands. Purge operates with its narrow internal deleted-account authority, not the person's restored initiator or `trustedAccount` (which currently requires active state).
3. **Close execution and classify frozen resources.** Persist stop/fence state for bulk and maintenance first. Resolve in-flight target outcomes using exact existing receipts; an unknown outcome remains unknown, never inferred from current rows. Collect every account-owned Collection, including already-deleted rows, in immutable-id pages; save retain/erase disposition and outstanding remote cleanup locators natively. Retained public resources are read-only, with no ownership transfer. Profile Collections visibility remains closed by Access; direct anonymous Collection reads, public subject listings and sitemap discovery continue under their existing resource/gate checks. Published logs remain only their snapshots.
4. **Settle public membership before destroying its evidence.** For erased Collections, make source eligibility irreversibly false (a Collection tombstone is sufficient for new source reads), capture bounded subject targets, and durably remove target pairs/count contributions. Existing `work-member-subjects.sql`/`listing.ts` provide useful bounded source enumeration and idempotent current-truth sync, but not the whole purge protocol. Current-member enumeration misses obsolete pairs for removed members; existing pair PKs are subject-leading. Recommend a Collection-leading reverse seek index on public pairs, bounded visits of existing subject partitions, and native phase/cursor/acks, rather than scanning a partition's entire pair table. Never create a subject-partition directory of private shelf holders.
5. **Fence late projection observations.** `syncSubjectListing` reads source, releases that connection, then applies on the subject partition. Its digest includes source transaction id/multiplicity, but `listing-apply.sql` has no monotonic source comparison. A delayed formerly-public positive observation can therefore race a terminal zero; a duplicate workflow id alone is not a freshness fence. Before a removal sweep/ack, establish a durable per-Collection terminal-removal fence on each relevant target partition, checked by **all** listing apply paths, so later stale positives cannot recreate erased pairs. Use a separate stable final-source/version fence and reconciliation for retained frozen public Collections, without deleting their valid pairs. Account for already in-flight member/listing events and newly discovered partitions before declaring exhaustion. A native reverse sweep plus target fence covers obsolete pairs; keeping members until merely enqueueing a listing job does not. Fence retention/collection must cover the replay horizon or continue to reject from permanent terminal source truth.
6. **Erase private domain payloads in child-first phases.** Invalidate reservations and other producers, then delete their staged text; erase entries/copies/follows, each session's units/history then session, erased Collections' members then Collection, all heads/counters/build generations and dictionary references/nodes/values in actual FK order. For nodes shared between reservations, enumerate the whole account namespace, not just active reservations. Detach retained logs' private source pointers under the adopted nullable schema. Maintain a parent cursor plus child cursor so one large session/Collection cannot turn a parent-page delete into an unbounded child cascade. Retained public Collection counts are not private shelf rollups to keep.
7. **Collect temporary evidence without reopening work.** Honor the adopted bulk evidence window and obtain target-side transfer/receipt-cleanup acknowledgements before removing their source locators. Clear backfill/rebuild/purge payloads and intents after their dependent phases; keep a minimal terminal marker (account id, tombstone binding, terminal epoch/run and completion state), not erased subject lists or text. New purge receipts contain only bounded phase/sequence/count/receipt locators, never deleted row bodies. Shared command/event/workflow owners must separately report their minimized/expired or still-held evidence. An unresolved target receipt or custody gap is visible and monitored, not a fabricated success.
8. **Checkpoint effects and continuation together.** Each transaction seeks an indexed immutable keyset with a fixed raw-row limit (100 is a reasonable starting proposal), changes at most that page's rows, and atomically commits phase cursor/sequence plus effects/acks and a durable continuation intent. Add separate byte/input budgets for large text and step results; row limits alone do not bound trie text/TOAST work. Use deterministic run/segment/page ids, bounded workflow segments and a recovery scan of runnable native intents. Crash before commit retries; crash after commit returns the native receipt/current cursor; enqueue-before-ack repeats the same segment. Workflow success, an outbox checkpoint and an enqueue receipt are each insufficient substitutes for native phase completion.
9. **Complete only after indexed exhaustion.** Under the terminal epoch, prove empty seeks for every erased namespace/generation and every remote removal/transfer phase; preserve the explicitly retained public set. Commit a Library-native completion receipt and final `purged` state atomically. The deleted Access account remains terminal forever. A native-domain completion receipt must enumerate any separately outstanding shared-evidence retention; an overall claim that Library-linked private data is physically erased must wait for that evidence too or explicitly name the exception. There is no implemented completion deadline here. Monitor age/backlog, stalled/missing events, expired worker custody and target failures; retry/repair never restores authority.

Overall work is linear in the erased account's rows/bytes plus bounded visits to relevant partitions and remote pairs, not constant per deletion. Per-page seeks need owner/operation/Collection/session-leading indexes, with no `OFFSET`, whole-account count, whole-tree sort, or unbounded result array. Future verification must exercise T-versus-writer ordering, all deletion paths, duplicate events, reservation/rebuild resurrection attempts, large history/partial tries, every commit/enqueue/ack crash, stale public positive observations, obsolete pairs, target-side transfer remnants and repeated purge completion. These are implementation requirements for adoption, not tests claimed to pass by this report.

## Adoption and actual dependencies

- **Library retention owner:** adopt the classification table in `src/library/README.md` Privacy and a retention inventory, including unlisted/member-note decisions, inert public attribution, snapshots and physical cleanup boundaries. Amend shelf/bulk owning contracts only for concrete gate/erasure binding, evidence custody and any changed replay promise. Define the nullable published-log source pointer and retained-resource mutation fence. This report remains evidence rather than a second authority.
- **Access/event producer:** the companion contract must cover every active tombstone path with the same in-barrier event, including organization closure and paths without a deletion continuation. That is the upstream prerequisite for the durable consumer; no new product retention decision or synchronous dependency is needed from Access. Reconcile older shelf/maintenance wording that conflates account deletion completion with later physical purge completion.
- **Library purge/wiring owner:** register the consumer and own native page/segment state, all applied tables, shelf dictionary/generation gates, bulk/target evidence hooks, listing reverse cleanup and late-positive fencing. Shelf backfill/rebuild/type-reservation and bulk owners must use the same terminal guard; they cannot independently ship an unfenced producer and call purge complete. Their final table/locator mapping is a real dependency, not proof supplied by a stub.
- **Command/event/durable-work owners:** adopt shared evidence minimization/retention and exact cleanup locators while preserving consumer delivery and replay correctness. Library native erasure can land independently with a truthful limited completion receipt; a blanket physical-erasure guarantee cannot land before these custody gaps are resolved. No broad shared-schema change is authorized by this research.
- **Privacy/confirmation owner:** publish and name the privacy contact; update the fresh deletion confirmation with public versus unlisted outcomes, note/log retention, pre-deletion removal and asynchronous cleanup/evidence limits. Review retention basis, identifying text, contribution licence/attribution and assisted removal after deletion. Separate statutory request handling, justified holds and backup/restore policy from product deletion; do not make them await the Library consumer.
