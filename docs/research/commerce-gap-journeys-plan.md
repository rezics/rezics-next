### Ownership and scope exclusions in the same report

- **Rank 3** people/producer/character properties and **rank 6** source vocabulary/release fidelity are
  Semantic/Imports/Web work, not additional Library field stores.
- **Rank 4** music/live-action profile publication and provider subject-type mapping remain with their existing
  Semantic/Content/Imports owners. Commerce consumes actual admitted profiles and reports unsupported imported
  types explicitly through the existing portability journeys.
- **Rank 5** classification ballots, source aggregate figures and native rating authority belong to Content
  and its catalogue/import consumers. A private imported score never authorizes a native vote or public figure.
- **Rank 8** optional visual graph and domain browsing are conditional reader surfaces. The report says a visual
  graph is not a basic parity blocker; it assigns no new Commerce journey.
- **Rank 9** authenticated Steam observations, AniDB file matching and other-provider specialized integrations
  require their own rights/fidelity contracts. Existing manual copies do not implement those integrations.
  Structured Discogs condition/custom-copy fields and provider activity synchronizers are likewise not claimed
  by uploaded-list, diary or native-copy coverage. The report places expansion beyond the launch promise after
  the first six findings; this allocation does not adopt that expansion as new Commerce scope.
- Live provider linking/synchronization, a challenge engine, recommendations, launchers and external marketplaces
  are not inferred from upload, year-summary or payment functionality. Their absence remains visible rather than
  being disguised as successful native implementation.

integrated assignment. Reissuing those journeys would duplicate the producers and interfaces the contract
requires this plan to preserve. The two running native assignments retain their whole journeys; integrated
assignments retain any documented unavailable seam and controller-owned verification.
