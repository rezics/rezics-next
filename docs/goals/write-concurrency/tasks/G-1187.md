## Outcome

G-1166 made ordinary commands share scope gates. One serialization point
remains, and it is the most frequent one.

- Real Realm membership transitions (self-join, accepted invitations and
  requests, joins, leaves, member administration) advance
  `access.scope_gate 'work:create:root'.authority_epoch`.
- Grant creation, role bindings, representations and member-set changes do the
  same.
- Every admission under that scope checks that epoch. So these writers take
  the global row FOR UPDATE, and every other Access command waits while one
  join commits.

G-1166's classification is in
`.temp/manager/write-concurrency/scope-gate-classification.md` (main checkout);
read it first.

**Decision (manager, 2026-10-06):** an authority change must invalidate only
the admissions that relied on what it changed.
- A join to Realm X can change who may act in Realm X. It must not invalidate
  an admission that relied on nothing in Realm X.
- Give these changes epoch domains no wider than what they change: the Realm,
  the granted scope, the Agent's representation, the member set.
- Have each admission prove, at claim and seal, that the exact authority rows
  its proof used are unchanged (generation or revision per row; the admission
  already records a proof digest), rather than checking one global counter.
- The global `work:create:root` epoch stays only for changes that genuinely
  affect every admission under it, such as closing the scope.

Correctness first: every existing revocation and epoch race test must keep
passing, and add the missing ones:
- a membership revoked between register and seal makes the seal fail when the
  admission used that membership;
- the same revocation does not fail an admission that did not use it.

Smallest change that achieves this: no new services and no policy-language
changes. Add tables only for a per-domain generation that does not already
exist.
