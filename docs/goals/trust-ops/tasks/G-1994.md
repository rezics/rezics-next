## Outcome

Class A, decided by Program (06:01). G-1967 (on main when this starts: read its commits 269bf8c11 and 1b5be98f2 and its tests first) fixed the sole-controller Account deletion blocker, but its third land review left three P2 findings that a design here may not leave, because deletion cost and interference must never grow with Agents whose authority the deletion does not change, and a co-controlled Person with a long chain built by someone else must not be able to stop another person's deletion.

## The rule (decided; implement exactly this)

1. Classify ONLY Agents whose LAST live controller is the deleting principal. CUT the inventory at any Agent that has another live controller (another principal or a live edge from a surviving Agent): it is not affected, not listed, not fenced, not walked beyond. Deleting A while B also controls Person P with a long downstream chain beneath P: the walk stops at P (P keeps its controller B); cost is independent of the chain.
2. The classification frontier is PERSISTED per step: each bounded step (a fixed row budget per statement, well inside the five-second statement timeout) extends the classified closure from the recorded frontier and commits its progress under the original deletion operation; a retry resumes from the frontier instead of recomputing; a long admitted chain classifies across steps, not in one statement, with no fixed-point rescans of already classified Agents (one new level per step at most O(new level), not O(chain)).
3. Person termination is ATOMIC with the principal deactivation also beyond 256 Agents: the principal's deactivation commits only together with the termination of every sole-controlled Person (the Persons that have to end) in the same transaction, and only AFTER classification completed; the intermediate states (classification steps, orphan batches for owned non-Person Agents) leave the principal ACTIVE and the Account intact (no state where Access is disabled while a Person stays active with zero controllers). The orphan recording of consented owned Agents may remain batched; the interruption of those batches must leave a consistent resumable state and never a Person without controllers.
4. The typed refusal, the 16-item preview with `moreCount`, the consent field and the 409 body stay exactly as on main.

## Acceptance (three failing tests first; write them red on main's code, then fix; say so in the handoff with both QA ids)

(a) INTERRUPT BEYOND 256: more than 256 Agents affected; interrupt (fault injection) after the classification and between batches: Access stays enabled (principal active), the Account and credentials intact, no Person active with zero controllers at any observed point; the retry completes with exactly one termination effect per Person. (b) CONSTANT COST: deleting A where an unaffected co-controlled Person chain (controlled also by B) grows at three sizes (e.g. 8, 64, 512): the deletion's statement count, rows read and wall-independent work are identical at the three sizes (assert counters, not time) and none of the unaffected chain is fenced or touched. (c) STATEMENT BUDGET: a long admitted Person chain solely controlled by the deleting principal classifies within the statement budget per step across several steps (assert each step stays under the budget with a counting/timeout-armed client and the total steps grow linearly), and a retry after a failed step resumes from the persisted frontier. Keep G-1967's tests green (the case that expected a partial state beyond 256 is replaced by (a)).
