## Outcome

Account recovery returns the account to its owner and locks out whoever held
its old factors.

Today a completed recovery claim (`recovery-claim.ts`, around line 270) does
three things:

- replaces the password;
- deletes sessions, OAuth tokens and consents;
- refuses accounts without a password credential.

Passkeys and TOTP stay bound. Whoever stole a passkey can still sign in, and an
owner who lost their second factor has no way to rebuild it.

After a completed claim:

1. **Every pre-recovery factor is revoked in the claim's transaction.** That
   covers:
   - passkeys and TOTP secrets, with their backup codes;
   - pending passwordless or magic links and reset verifications;
   - sessions, OAuth access and refresh tokens, and consents.
   An old passkey, an old TOTP code or an old session fails afterwards.
2. **The owner rebinds at least one factor in the recovery flow.** That can be
   a password, a passkey or passwordless email, whichever the account had or
   chooses. Accounts without a password credential recover too.
3. **The claim stays idempotent.**
   - A retried request after a lost response returns the same outcome without
     revoking again.
   - A replayed or used recovery code is refused.
   - The recovery generation advances once.
4. **The Accounts UI completes the flow.** It shows what recovery removed and
   asks the owner to add a factor, in the Google Account style the Accounts site
   already follows.

## Do not build

- No second identity provider.
- No session-only fix.
- No change to the guardian approval rule. Guardian consent is the next task
  (T4), which builds on this one.
- Leave the email-existence signal in recovery enrollment to T4.
- Use the auth library's own factor tables and APIs. Do not build a parallel
  factor store.
