## Outcome

Two CLASS A defects of the disclosure contract that landed at 12:04 (G-1993), found by the third review of Kernel's G-1972 and decided by Program (12:27). They decide WHEN the strict contract may be switched on for an owner.

(a) THE 64-TARGET LIMIT AGAINST CONSUMERS THAT SUBMIT MORE: the sitemap (up to 501 targets), discloseSearchMatches (up to 512), notifications (digest/inbox pages). Under the strict contract these throw (`requireBatch`). RULE: the contract owns the bounded batching: ONE public rule, stated in the contract header: a submission of N targets is processed as ceil(N/64) bounded submissions of at most 64 targets each (owner statements per chunk exactly as DISCLOSURE_COST says), results returned in input order as one result, one cost report summing the chunks; an overall cap (state it, e.g. 1,024 per call) beyond which the call is refused before any owner work; chunks are submitted sequentially on the same snapshot/cut rules (a cut is per capture; the final fence rechecks every chunk). Consumers do not chunk by themselves any more. Applies to read, capture, resolve and fence entries (disclose, discloseInventory, captureDisclosure, fenceDisclosure) uniformly via one helper. Tests: 65, 501, 512 and 1,024 targets succeed with the right chunk counts and unchanged decisions; 1,025 refused before owner work; cost accounting equals measured (statement counts measured on ALL statements); order and duplicates preserved.

(b) THE FINAL FENCE COMPARES THE GLOBAL ENFORCEMENT CUT: `fenceDisclosure` compares the global enforcement cut (access.site_moderation_basis()), so a restriction acknowledged on an UNRELATED Work between capture and fence fails the read (the rule removed from Work reads today in G-1972). RULE: the fence compares the enforcement cut of the TARGETS IT CAPTURED and nothing wider: the cut basis recorded at capture is derived from the enforcement rows that apply to the captured selections and their parents (target, parent Work, parent Realm and the Realm-closure restriction), e.g. the maximum version/acknowledgement token over exactly those rows (one indexed statement per chunk), and the fence recomputes that SAME scoped basis on a fresh snapshot; equal -> proceed; changed -> unavailable (a restriction on one of THESE targets moved). The recovery generation (global) stays part of the fence. Tests on real Access/Governance rows: a restriction acknowledged on an unrelated Work between capture and fence -> ZERO replays and the read proceeds; a restriction on the captured Work, on its parent Work, on its parent Realm, and a whole-Realm closure -> the fence refuses; two-parent descriptor cases; statement counts constant.
