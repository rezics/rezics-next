## Outcome

MEASURED on the shared development dataset (read-only EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS), 11,085 retained batches, 9,309 events, one epoch): `main.discovery.refresh` fails on EVERY poll, about once a minute, with `WorkReadUnavailable` `work_read_deadline_exceeded` then `RecommendationStale`. The read is the coverage statement in `services/main/src/modules/discovery/source.ts` (lines ~91-128): its `coverage` CTE computes `COALESCE((SELECT max(b.sequence) FROM relay.delivered_batch b WHERE ... b.sequence <= c.sequence AND EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM relay.delivered_event e WHERE ... e.envelope #>> '{data,sourcePosition,sequence}' ... <= $5)),0)` and the planner places that subquery in the index condition of the batch join, so it is RE-EVALUATED FOR EVERY OUTPUT ROW (up to the 257-row budget) and each evaluation scans every event of the epoch and parses its envelope JSON. Measured 22 to 32 seconds and 10 to 14 million buffer hits per statement at this population (the plan is staged at `.temp/ref/discovery-explain.txt`). The cost is proportional to RETAINED HISTORY times the page: class A by the scale rule. The same history grows without bound.

Since relay migrations 024/025 every retained header carries its graph cut (`relay.delivered_batch.graph_sequence`, indexed on `(stream_scope, data_epoch, graph_sequence)` by 025; see `docs/contracts/events-and-jobs.md` and `services/main/src/modules/outbox/repair-graph-cut.ts`). The coverage position "the latest EVENT-BEARING batch whose graph position is at most the discovery cut" is therefore ONE indexed backward lookup on the header: `max(sequence)` over headers with `event_count > 0` and `graph_sequence <= $5` and the same scope/epoch/`<= coverage.sequence`, without touching `delivered_event` at all. Do exactly that:

1. Growth check FIRST (Main test on the real relay Postgres schema with synthetic headers/events at 1,000 / 4,000 / 16,000 batches; assert through `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` of the production statement that shared-buffer hits for the coverage lookup do NOT grow with history (bounded index probes) and that the old text shows the growth: commit it first as `test.failing` and state the measured numbers before the fix).
2. Replace the correlated EXISTS with the header lookup, evaluated ONCE (a materialized CTE or a lateral with LIMIT 1 ordered by sequence DESC), keeping every semantic of the statement (source `https://rezics.com/services/main`, product dataset and epoch checks are properties of the event envelopes: if any of them cannot be expressed on the header, prove from `outbox/relay.ts` `deliver()` that every event of an event-bearing Main batch satisfies them, or keep the cheap per-batch check ONLY for the one boundary batch the lookup returns). A header with a NULL graph cut (legacy, not yet repaired) must not be silently treated as "before": refuse explicitly naming the bulk repair (`task main:relay -- --repair-graph-cuts`), the same way `feed/source.ts` does.
3. Flip the check to a plain test; add an integration check that the discovery refresh completes within its deadline on a store with 16,000 retained batches (isolated QA stack seeded with relay rows through the relay's own `deliver`/`retainBatch`, not raw SQL on the product tables).
4. Say in your result the new buffer counts and the statement's wall time at the largest size.
