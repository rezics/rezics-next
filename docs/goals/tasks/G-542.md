## Outcome
Every channel that can disclose a resource checks the same two things through one
batched call:
1. the governance restriction fence;
2. G-509's suitability eligibility for this viewer and channel.

The channels are:
- direct reads and summaries;
- Realm threads and the feed;
- search results, typeahead and counts;
- inbox entries and digests;
- public previews, SEO metadata and the sitemap;
- exports;
- media delivery.

When a decision restricts a target, that target disappears from all of them. The
target may be a Work name, a Content body, a post or reply, or a media asset.
Restoring it brings it back. An r18 or r18g target is never sent by email or
push, and anonymous previews and indexing see only what an anonymous viewer may.
Unknown results fail closed. This is foundation 9
(`docs/operations/security.md#one-disclosure-and-enforcement-policy`) for launch.

## What to read
- `modules/governance/store.ts:640-685`: `access.governance_enforcement`, `restrictedTitles` and `restrictedContentRevision`.
- Callers of those readers:
  - `work/read-session.ts:127`, `media/summary.ts:383`, `search-disclosure/public-fields.ts`, `search/fields.ts`;
  - `routes/{resources,search,content}.ts`.
  - All of them are optional (`governance?.store`), so they fail open when the store is not wired.
- `content/src/moderation.ts` writes `content.moderation_effect`, and nothing reads it.
- Effect labels in `governance/schema.ts:18`.
- G-509: the `eligible()` policy and `POST /v1/suitability/reads`. It says "M5 wires it into delivery"; this brief is that wiring.
- G-506's target resolver.
- Viewer evidence comes from G-731, the M8 sign-up brief: birth month, request country, and Account's introspection claim. Until it merges, age is `unknown`, which fails closed.

## Approach
- Add `modules/disclosure/`, one batched function. For each target it returns `visible | tombstone | hidden`, given `(targets, viewer, channel)`.
  - It reads the fence rows for the channel's effect labels (disclosure, search, delivery, export).
  - It calls G-509's policy with the viewer, or the anonymous viewer for index and preview channels.
- Viewer: `{ signedIn, age, country, optIns }`.
  - Age and adult eligibility come from Account's introspection claims (G-731).
  - Country comes from `CF-IPCountry`, which the web BFF forwards. Main trusts it only on the first-party BFF path.
  - Opt-ins stay unavailable until their owner exists.
- Make `governance` a required dependency: `routes/dependencies.ts` and `index.ts` are union-merged, add-only. Enforce in the modules the routes call, and keep route files out of scope.
- Call the function at each channel's final assembly point, in batches under existing cost budgets:
  - Reads, threads and feed replace the item with a non-disclosing tombstone that keeps its structural position.
  - Search excludes before counts and facets.
  - Notifications recheck at display and delivery, not only at enqueue.
  - SEO, previews and the sitemap use Main's anonymous result and never cache a restricted body.
  - Exports record an explicit omission.
  - Media delivery follows the asset state that G-571 and G-565 maintain.

## Do not build
- No rule language or policy engine, and no second suitability or fence query per channel.
- No spoiler-position gating: the wiki area owns it.
- No blocks: that is deferred G-545.
- No cache-purge infrastructure. State plainly that bytes already delivered cannot be recalled.
- No route-file edits.

## Acceptance
- A channel-matrix integration test uses one list of channels and five fixtures: a Work name, a body, a reply, a post and a media asset. For each fixture:
  1. read it on every channel;
  2. restrict it through decide; assert it is absent or a tombstone everywhere, including counts and notification payloads;
  3. restore it; assert it is back.
- Repeat the matrix with an r18 assessment for an anonymous viewer, a signed-in viewer without evidence, and email/push.
- Class guard: removing the call from any channel, or making the dependency optional, fails the matrix.
- Browser: a restricted Work's share URL, opened in a fresh browser, shows neither the name nor its metadata.
