## Outcome

Read-only design pass; change no code. Class A (program, 2026-10-12 04:04 UTC).
Nobody can take a Realm out of public reach today (launch's analysis is staged
at `.temp/ref/g1963-realm-lifecycle.md`):
- the owner has no retire;
- the platform has no takedown;
- a moderation or rights restriction aimed at a Realm fails validation, because
  a Realm is checked against the Work shape;
- a restriction does not reach the Realm's threads, replies or attached Zones,
  because enforcement matches an exact resource;
- Realm directory search reads the summary channel, so a search restriction
  does not cover it.

Design one mechanism for both halves, so they do not diverge: a Realm state
"out of public reach", with
- (a) the owner's retire and recover, on the Zone lifecycle precedent
  (`services/main/src/modules/zone/configuration.ts` retirement and recovery);
- (b) the platform's takedown and restoration, as a moderation and rights
  effect through the existing Governance and notice path.

The design states:
1. **For each surface** (Realm home, threads, replies, roster, attached Zones,
   search and feeds, links into the Realm): what an anonymous reader, a member
   and a manager see in each state (public, retired, taken down, restored).
   Hidden equals absent where a reader must not learn it exists.
2. **How the state reaches the Realm's descendants** (threads, replies, attached
   Zones, ranking) at read time without a population-sized walk at decision
   time: bounded, the scale rule.
3. **What a Realm decision validates against** in place of the Work shape.
4. **What Realm directory search enforces** and through which channel.
5. **Owners and files:**
   - launch: the owner lifecycle and the read side in the realm modules;
   - trust-ops: the effect in Governance, validation and inheritance;
   - Access: if a scope gate is involved.
   Reuse existing mechanisms (the Zone lifecycle, Governance effects, scope
   gates, disclosure). No new domain backend.
6. **Erasure interaction:** what happens to a retired or taken-down Realm when
   its owner's Account is deleted, given the last-controller refusal.
7. **An acceptance table at the end:** each row a state × surface × reader, with
   the exact expected answer and the test that will prove it.

Trust-ops reviews this design before any implementation.

## Narrowed by decision 56 (maintainer, 2026-10-12 04:07 UTC)

See `docs/contracts/space.md` "Closing a Realm" and `docs/product/decisions.md`.
A Realm gets a takedown channel, and for now ONLY the platform's. An owner does
not close a Realm at will; that may come later. Making a Realm private is a
separate matter, and today's visibility settings stay as they are.
- **In scope:**
  - the platform's closure and restoration of a whole Realm, as a moderation
    and rights effect;
  - what an anonymous reader, a member and a manager see on each surface while
    the Realm is closed (home, threads, replies, roster, attached Zones, search
    and feeds, links in);
  - how the effect reaches the Realm's threads, replies and attached Zones
    without walking the population at decision time;
  - what a decision on a Realm validates against in place of the Work shape;
  - Realm directory search honouring the closure;
  - the indexes draining: G1042's two cases will prove the platform closure.
- **Out of scope:** the owner's retire, recover or delete, and any change to
  private visibility. Do not design them, and leave no hooks for them beyond
  what the closed state needs anyway.

Trust-ops owns the effect and accepts the design; launch takes the read side in
the realm modules. Drop the owner half (a) from your design and the acceptance
table.
