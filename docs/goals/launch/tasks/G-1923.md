## Outcome

Class A (program, 2026-10-11 22:30 UTC). A published Zone home page is
user-published content that nobody can report and no rights holder can name in
a notice. A restriction on it lands on the whole site, because the document and
the Zone share one identity. The design is staged at
`.temp/ref/g1921-home-identity.md`; the program accepted its part (a).

The identity:
- One stable native Resource **P** per home document, separate from the Zone
  **Z** and shared by its language variants.
- One immutable native revision anchor **H** per published document cut, which
  keeps the exact variant, revision and digest selections and its predecessor.
- Edits keep P; publication advances H. Discussion roots stay on their original
  H.
- Zone publication owns Z→P. Nothing existing is rewritten: assign lazily,
  through the publication owner. There are 16 Zones and none published.
- Reuse the existing graph, manifests, revision anchors, Content custody and
  receipts. No new domain backend.

One chain, one branch, each step a resume, landing once:
1. **Zone publication, Content and Access identity.**
   - The publication owner allocates P lazily and publishes P and H atomically
     with the site bundle, replacing the `page === Zone` and
     Content-owner-equals-Zone checks (`configuration.ts:376,462`).
   - New variants use P as resourceId. The server derives the owning Zone for
     `zone.edit` authorization.
   - Settlement proves that the Zone receipt selected P's exact bytes
     (`content-publication/publish.ts:560`).
   - Owner and integration tests: stale publication, revoked authority,
     multilingual selection, retained roots, and retry recovery.
2. **Target resolution and report or notice admission.** Do not start this step
   before trust-ops has answered whether P and H meet its conditions for a
   notice, report and erasure target; the manager resumes you with that
   answer.
   - Expose P as `base: resource`, with visibility and the exact H from the
     publication owner (`media/summary.ts`, `target/resolve.ts`).
   - Admit home documents in public reports and notices
     (`public-report/owners.ts:42,202`) and the authenticated evidence path,
     checking the Zone's read authority for a home document
     (`governance/composition.ts:23`).
   - A restriction is scoped to the document, not the site.
   - Trust-ops reviews this step's diff before landing.
   - These files are trust-ops' area; the manager widens the claim with
     `--allow-area` for this step.
3. **The editor, and the discussion on home frames.**
   - The editor carries the Zone and the document separately
     (`apps/web/features/zone-editor/api.ts:57`).
   - Unhide the discussion entry on home frames, so it is about P.
   - A browser proof of a published home page that a reader reports, and
     discusses through its attached Realm.

Out of scope (B, listed for the maintainer): site publications in search, a
traversable home history, and comments, reviews, ratings, Statements, export
and following on a home page.

## Trust-ops' conditions (2026-10-11 22:50 UTC; staged at `.temp/ref/trust-ph-conditions.md`)

Trust-ops accepts P and H as a notice, report and erasure target on these
conditions. It reviews step 2's diff before it lands. Step 2 lists which
condition each of its tests covers.

**Step 1:**
- **(5) Restore.** P and H use only the reference shapes Content recovery
  coverage already pins for graph-to-Content references (`rv:contentRevision`
  on an anchor, with the retained digest). A new shape goes into the coverage
  owner in this branch, with a tamper test.
- **(6) No authority from P.** The owning Zone is derived on the server for
  `zone.edit`. A Work-edit grant cannot edit a home page, nor the reverse; one
  Access scope test each way.
- **(7) Lazy migration.** Nothing existing is rewritten. A legacy variant whose
  resourceId is the Zone stays resolvable for notices and erasure until the
  Zone's next save. State and test that.
- **(4, part) Erasure.** H is a `GRAPH_ERASURE_KINDS` member, or is suppressed
  through an existing kind, so that erasing a revision suppresses every H that
  selects it and the digest it carries. The erasure inventory finds every such
  H; state the bound (at most one H per publication cut that selected it). A
  home page whose selected revision is erased reads as absent for that cut, not
  as an empty page.

**Step 2:**
- **(1) Name = (P, H), exact.** The resolver returns the Content revisions H
  selects, exactly.
  - The URL grammar admits P's `/e/` address.
  - A Zone `/z/` display URL resolves to (P, the observed H).
  - Only published H is nameable, never a draft.
  - A notice never discloses content outside the exact H.
- **(2) The restriction binds the document.** It binds P (every H and every
  language variant) or one exact H, never Z.
  - The Zone, its navigation and its other pages keep serving.
  - Restoration lifts exactly that restriction.
  - A restricted home page reads like an absent page, with the same answer and
    cost shape, for the Zone's own readers too.
- **(3) Evidence admission** checks the Zone's read authority. A normal reader
  can complete a report; a non-reader cannot learn the page exists.
