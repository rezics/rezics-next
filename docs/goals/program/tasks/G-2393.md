## Outcome

The supermanager knows from measurement what the hours from 21:50 UTC on 2026-10-13 to 00:48 UTC on 2026-10-14 cost for each landed task, where the attempts that landed nothing went, and which change gains the most landed tasks an hour now. The staged `.temp/ref/efficiency-audit-5.txt` reads the sessions begun in that window: 242 sessions, 212 workers, 5,415 steps, 354.4M input tokens, 96% cached; workers by step read 37%, wait 34%, edit 12%, test 6%. `.temp/ref/efficiency-audit-4-researcher.md` is the fourth reading; count this window the same way so the five windows compare. You change no file of the repository.

What the supermanager found in the window, to test against the records and not to take on trust:

(a) Attempts that end in a report. `.temp/ref/efficiency-audit-5-attempts.txt` counts from the ledger, for each Goal, attempts started, how their handoffs ended and what landed. api: 53 ended attempts, 12 `done`, 24 `partial`, 11 `blocked`; 18 tasks landed on 53 attempts. program: 27 landed on 38. web: 7 on 15, with 12 attempts that left no handoff file. At 00:19 UTC the program sent api a standing rule (diagnosis and repair are one task; a worker is bounded by claim and test budget, not by a count of runs; a wrong answer against a settled contract is briefed as its repair).
(b) Tasks that wait on one exited task. At 00:41 UTC 33 briefs were queued. Their roots were exited tasks waiting for a manager: G-2220 (exited 00:09, holds G-2218, G-2268, G-2271 and through them three program record tasks and web's surfaces), G-2307 (23:50), G-2304 (00:14), G-2234 of web (20:40), and G-2323 still running with G-2355 and G-2380 behind it. Operations had no worker running and four exited tasks.
(c) The harness on main. Two regressions made unit gates fail for every Goal until the program fixed them: the admission clock read before the dev bypass (in main from `7eec2dca7` until `a877463e1` at about 00:26) and three slot tests of `tests/qa/unit/qa-harness.test.ts` (until `64dc60068` at 00:37).
(d) The host. Memory bounds the stacks: 62 GiB of RAM, five to seven stacks, three to five runs waiting for memory at a time, one-minute load 35 to 72. The proxy's process lookup was switched off at 22:25 (CPU pressure 31% to 6%).
(e) Record tasks. A record's production reads shared the proof's 60 s test limit (G-2337: three runs, five personas at 43 to 57 s, the sixth at the limit, no request over 6 s); a record cannot publish while one cell of its row is pending.

Read in the main checkout (read only): workers' `.temp/goal-orchestration/runs/<id>/attempt-<n>.json`, `attempt-<n>.last.md`, `settle.log` and `land.log`; the ledger `.temp/goal-orchestration/ledger.json`; the Goals' mail under `.temp/goal-orchestration/` as far as it is readable; each worktree's QA artifacts under `.temp/worktrees/<id>/.artifacts/qa/`; `git log` of `main` for the landings; and `scripts/goal/efficiency.ts` for what the reading means. Write `.temp/goal/audit.md` in your worktree, at most ninety lines:

1. **The five windows, counted one way.** The four of the fourth reading and 21:50 to 00:48 UTC: worker input, manager input, tasks landed, input for each landed task, workers with a live process (median), landings an hour.
2. **Where the attempts went.** For each Goal in this window: attempts for each landed task, and the share of ended attempts whose handoff was not `done`. Then class every `partial` and `blocked` attempt of api and of program by what stopped it: its brief forbade the repair or the rerun; it asked the program a question the contract already answered; it asked one the contract did not answer; a host or tool failure; another task's claim; a real fault of the work. Give the count, the worker input and the waiting time to the next attempt for each class. Say which class the 00:19 rule removes and whether the attempts started after 00:19 show it.
3. **What waited on a manager.** For the roots named in (b) and any other you find: how long each sat exited before its manager's next action on it, how many queued or exited tasks stood behind it, and the task-hours that waited. For each of the four managers: the median and the longest time from a worker's exit to the manager's next command on that task.
4. **Gates and stacks.** Landing attempts that failed on (c) alone; stack runs ended, passed, failed on the host alone, and repeated; minutes runs waited for memory. Say what one more stack would be worth and what it would need.
5. **Three changes, the largest gain in landed tasks an hour first.** For each: the measure now, the expected measure after, where it is made, what it risks. Then what is not worth changing.

The handoff repeats sections 1, 2, 3 and 5 in at most forty-five lines.

Do not build: a change to any file of the repository, a new tool, advice without a measure.
