## Outcome

When an outcome test runs into its limit, its log does not say which request it was waiting for. Four tasks this evening (2026-10-13) came back with a time-out nobody could classify: the Recipes exchange's stranger at 60.000 s with "the exact pending request and internal phase times absent"; the Books hub record ("the log identifies no outstanding request"); a phrase query whose phrase and answer were not kept; the Books browse record's fixture time-outs. Each then cost a ruling, a second run and often a third, because host and product cannot be told apart without the request.

What must be true afterwards:

1. Every request a persona's client sends (`call` in `tests/qa/support/personas.ts`, to Main and to Account) is watched. The run's log gets one line when a request has been pending 10 s (persona, method, path with its query, elapsed), one line when a request that took longer than 5 s completes (the same, plus status and the answer's trace or request id header when it has one), and, when a test ends by failure or by its limit, one line for each request still in flight.
2. The lines are greppable by one fixed prefix and carry no bearer, cookie or body.
3. Nothing else changes: no limit, no retry, no ordering, no assertion. The watch costs nothing measurable per request (a timer and a map entry); say what you measured on one stack run.
4. The watch lives in its own small module with an injected clock and timer, and `tests/qa/unit/request-watch.test.ts` proves the three kinds of line, the absence of secrets, and that a completed request leaves nothing behind.
5. One stack run of `tests/qa/integration/outcomes/realm/create-a-realm.test.ts` proves it is wired: quote in the handoff the slow-request lines it printed, or say there were none and show the wiring by a second, deliberately held request in the unit test.

Do not build: a change of any time limit, a retry, a tracing system, a change of the outcome recorder or of any row test.
