## Outcome

On 2026-10-14 at 00:50 UTC nine surface tasks of one Goal had stood exited for two to seven hours. Their workers (Grok) had ended without writing a handoff, which happens when the session has grown too large, and nothing in `status` told the manager that these differed from tasks held on purpose. In the same hour two refusals cost a manager and the program a round each because they did not say what to do.

What must be true afterwards:

1. `status` marks an exited task whose last attempt left no handoff (no last-message file, or one without a `RESULT:` line) with one extra line: that the attempt ended without a handoff, for how long, and the command that helps: `resume <id> --fresh -m <text>`, or `--engine <engine>` to move it. A task that is `waiting` for a resume or has a settlement reason keeps the line it has.
2. `status` ends its task list with one line a manager can act on: how many of this Goal's tasks are exited, the three that have been exited longest with their age, and how many live worker places are free. With `GOAL_ID=program` it prints that line for every Goal. Nothing is printed when no task is exited.
3. `land` on a task in `conflict` whose stop is of kind `task` keeps refusing, and the refusal now names the remedy for the stop it holds: correct the brief, `reclaim <id> <brief>`, then `merge <id>`. Take the wording from what the code permits; if `merge` does not accept that state, say what does.
4. After two fix rounds `resume` looks for the decision in the last section whose heading begins with `## Decision`, so `## Decision — 2026-10-14 00:40 UTC: design note` is read like a bare `## Decision`. The date and the word (`cut`, `split`, `design note`) may be in the heading or the body. The refusal quotes the heading it looked at.
5. `scripts/goal/idle-tasks.test.ts` proves 1 to 4 on a temporary ledger, the way the tool's other tests build theirs, in well under a minute.

Do not build: an automatic resume, a notification, a change to what `reclaim` or `merge` check, or a new command.
