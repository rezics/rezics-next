## Outcome

A fault of the coordinator costs a wait, never work. What a pass starts (a worker the queue dispatches, a capacity continuation, a landing's settle wrapper) is a child of the pass and so runs in the service's control group, and the unit has `KillMode=control-group`: every exit of the service kills them. On 2026-10-13 the service's exit at 13:48:52 UTC killed a queued worker and a continuation in the same second (code 143; both were first read as sessions that ended by themselves); at 14:48 UTC two passes ended with `Event store statement "COMMIT" failed after waiting 5000 ms`, the service restarted twice, and the restarts killed a worker one minute old and a landing in its gate. `docs/development/toolchain.md` says the opposite ("killing the coordinator therefore leaves manager-dispatched workers running"). Since 14:54 UTC a loop of the supermanager moves such processes into a transient unit by hand; this task makes that loop unnecessary.

1. **A pass runs in a scope of its own.** `freshCoordinatorPass` (`scripts/goal/manager-commands.ts:259`) starts the pass process in a transient scope outside the service's group when the supervisor itself runs in a systemd unit (`systemd-run --user --scope --collect`, or the same through the bus), so whatever the pass starts inherits that scope and outlives the service. Outside systemd (a test, a shell) it starts the pass as today. Decide from the supervisor's own `/proc/self/cgroup`; the decision and the command line are a pure function in `scripts/goal/pass-scope.ts`, tested without systemd. If the scope cannot be made, the pass still runs, in the group, and one line says so.
2. **A failed pass ends no service.** The supervisor's loop (`:249`) prints one line for a pass that exits non-zero (its exit code, and the last line of its error) and runs the next pass after the interval. It does not exit, so `Restart=on-failure` is left for a fault of the supervisor itself. `coordinator status` shows the time and the line of the last failed pass. A pass still fails as a whole: do not catch faults inside it.
3. **The unit and the document say what happens.** `scripts/goal/goal-coordinator.service` keeps `KillMode=control-group` (a stop must end the supervisor and a pass that hangs in the group) and its comment names what lives outside the group and how; correct the paragraph of `docs/development/toolchain.md` that claims it today (near line 600).
4. **Proved on the real host, once, by hand.** In a throwaway user unit (`systemd-run --user --unit=<name>`, never `goal-coordinator.service`), run the supervisor's pass launch with a pass module that starts a `sleep`; stop the unit; show that the `sleep` lives and in which scope, then end it. Give the commands and their output in the handoff. Do not stop, restart or edit the installed coordinator unit: the program installs the template and restarts the service after this lands.

Do not build: a change to how a worker, a continuation or a settle wrapper is spawned (`launch.ts`, `settle.ts`, `queue.ts` are other tasks' files, and step 1 covers them by inheritance), a change of `KillMode`, a watchdog, a retry inside a pass, or a change to the event store's wait.

## Checks

`task goal -- check`, and the two test files once with `CLAUDECODE=1`. The tests cover: the scope decision for a supervisor in a service group, in a session scope and with no systemd; the supervisor's loop running the next pass after a pass that exits 1 and after one that exits by a signal, with the line it prints; a stop still ending the loop. Wait on a running command with one long call (`yield_time_ms: 300000`).
