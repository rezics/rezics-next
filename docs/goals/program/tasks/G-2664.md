## Outcome

The native contract pilot (`docs/architecture/api-contracts.md`) converted three operations. Each contract still repeats the responses that every operation with the same policy has. `services/main/src/routes/work-scalar.contract.ts` declares all of these by hand:

- 401 for a bearer route;
- 429 and the rate-limit 503s for a rate-limit family;
- 500 for every operation;
- the dependency-unavailable 503.

That repetition is most of why a pilot operation grew from 7–28 lines to about 70.

Derive those responses from the contract's declared policy. A contract then lists its success responses and its own domain failures, and nothing else. Declaring a policy adds its responses to the runtime validation, the generated OpenAPI and the Eden types.

- Use native Elysia and TypeBox composition, for example a helper that merges a policy's response map into the contract's `response`, or the existing policy macros. Do not build a routing layer.
- Keep G-2644's response coverage guard passing, and keep literal codes precise.
- Convert the three pilot contracts to this form.

Measure against the pilot's numbers with its runner: lines per contract, the instantiation counts from the inference gate, and the OpenAPI diff, which must be empty because only the declaration changes. Update the measured table in `docs/architecture/api-contracts.md`.
