# Content governance, reports and exact evidence

## Cases and authority

Reports, assessments, decisions, enforcement and appeals are distinct records.
Each case has a target, scope, exact evidence, applicable rule revision, responsible
authority and disclosure policy. Platform, Realm and resource-owner powers have
explicit independent permissions. A classification/rating is not a moderation
decision. Preserve other authorized contexts when one Realm rejects content.

## Exact reported content evidence

A report identifies the resource component and exact revision/representation
actually reported: body, title/name, structure, media use or another admitted grain.
Capture provenance and bounded evidence while the reporter is authorized. A current
head pointer or a copied preview alone cannot prove what was reported. Empty,
unavailable, erased and unsupported evidence remain distinct; no fabricated body
is created for metadata-only objects.

Reviewers receive only authorized evidence, source revision and current target
state. Historical readability is checked independently from current enforcement
authority. A report concerning old content cannot silently condemn a new revision
without an explicit decision scope. Bulk reports and appeals deduplicate intended
operations without merging independent evidence.

## Decision and enforcement lifecycle

Propose -> review -> decide -> apply -> appeal/reverse as appropriate. Bind the
decision to exact rule/evidence revisions and expected target state. Stale rule
or target changes return conflict/review-needed. Reversal is an attributable new
decision, never deletion of the old one. Concurrent reversals have one effect.

Enforcement can restrict disclosure, publication, participation or a specific
capability. Advance effective fences before bounded propagation to search, caches,
notifications and media. Deleting a thread root preserves other authors' posts and
their original targets. Large impact work is paged and recoverable.

## Rights complaints

The [source policy](source-lifecycle.md#basis-for-acquisition-and-reuse) favors
intake with provenance and later scoped complaint handling. Record the claimant,
claimed work/right, affected locations/components, supporting notice and receipt
time. Distinguish a complaint, interim access restriction and final disposition;
a complaint is not itself proof of infringement. Do not expand a claim about a
cover or synopsis into deletion of unrelated catalog facts.

Route ordinary disputes and applicable DMCA notices through their respective
processes. Where relying on § 512, act expeditiously on qualifying notices or
relevant infringement knowledge; an interim restriction need not await a final
merits decision. Track uploader notice, counter-notice and restoration under the
applicable process. The [legal review](../research/source-data-rights.md#complaint-handling-and-section-512)
records conditions and limits; it does not establish REZICS's safe-harbor eligibility
or treat platform-selected imports as user-directed storage.

Bind restrictions to the affected source/material/use and propagate them through
publication, source refresh, raw delivery, search, caches and media. An appeal or
counter-notice does not silently clear that fence. Restore only through an
attributable decision under the applicable process. Preserve independent facts
and permitted case evidence; use the erasure process for required physical deletion.

## Correction, erasure and qualification

Ownership intervention, identity merge/split and personal-data erasure use their
dedicated commands. Suppression, retraction and physical erasure are different.
Restoration validates current authority/rules and cannot revive erased payloads.
Qualify exact catalog/native reports, stale evidence, inaccessible components,
independent Realm outcomes, appeals, repeated effects and backup replay.
