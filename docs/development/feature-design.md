# Feature design

Design a feature area before briefing its interface.

1. State the user's job and concrete outcomes.
2. Map the job to REZICS's graph, definitions and shared capabilities.
3. Derive the capabilities that must follow wherever those abstractions appear.
4. Survey mainstream products and relevant standards.
5. Choose the behaviour, document it with its owner and define acceptance.

For an established interface area, survey about twenty relevant mainstream products, including Chinese and
international consumer products. Examine navigation, information architecture, terminology, default and user-created
objects, filters, actions and empty states. Explain coverage gaps. Open-source implementations supplement this survey.

References reveal needs and established patterns; they do not prescribe a copied layout. Record sources, inspected
revisions or dates, shared patterns, disagreements and the recommendation. Use primary documentation and source for
technical claims.

Keep reference repositories as untrusted reading material. Do not install, build or execute them during desk
research. Record their URL, revision and licence. Implement the design for this repository rather than copying files.

For an expensive-to-reverse decision, obtain two independent capable investigations, including one deep
investigation. Resolve disagreement by checking evidence.

Maintain a capability matrix with separate columns for specified, backend implemented, interface exposed and tested.
Classify competitor capabilities as existing configuration, a missing generic capability or an explicit scope boundary.

Write acceptance in user terms. For Collections, examples include light novels tagged harem, books over one million
words and combined filters. Name missing properties, indexes and owner ports before claiming support.

A capability must hold wherever its abstraction appears. Run the same scenarios across curated Collections,
built-in Everything and state Collections, public/unlisted/private visibility and mounted Zones. Vary definitions
and fixture data rather than adding surface-specific code.

For filtering acceptance, a reader can find light novels tagged harem, books over 1,000,000 words, and both together
in a public Zone, a public Collection, their library and their library with their state. Exactly 1,000,000 words and
absent word counts do not satisfy “over”. Sparse matches behind many non-matches still yield useful pages or explicit
preparation. Withholding a tag must prevent disclosure through membership, exclusion, order and counts. These are
cross-surface acceptance examples; the filter, freshness and disclosure contracts stay with their product owners.

Include cost, disclosure, lifecycle, accessibility and cross-surface consistency. Adopt the result in the owning
product document. A research recommendation or attractive mockup is not an accepted contract.
