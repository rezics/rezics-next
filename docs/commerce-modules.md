# Commerce modules: current boundary and future foundations

Owner: Commerce. This is the sole future Commerce module map, backed by the delivered [ownership and benefits research](research/commerce-ownership-entitlements-benefits.md) and [payment evidence and settlement research](research/commerce-payment-evidence-settlement.md). It does not replace the [current Commerce contract](../services/kernel/src/commerce/README.md), [scope](scope.md), [architecture](architecture.md), [Access](../services/kernel/src/access/README.md) or [Library](library-and-collections.md). Recommendations require manager adoption before runtime work.

**Adopted future foundation:** one double-entry ledger underlies future financial modules. **Current boundary:** no wallet, issued points/tokens, market, exchange, transferable-item implementation or ledger is introduced here. Current one-time offers, direct-account hosted Checkout, buyer orders/entitlements and creator statements remain the baseline. Points remain deferred. The current owner contract adopts one-time, account-bound, one immutable fixed-version benefit/quantity one and direct licensed sales by REZICS. Production eligibility/rights/setup remain EXTERNAL and unproved. The provider report supports separate payment, entitlement, provider cash and creator-liability evidence; it does not approve the business or settle pending commercial/shared choices.

## Separate things before separating modules

A **subject** identifies a public work. An **offer** specifies an acquisition proposal. An **order** captures accepted business facts. **Payment evidence** describes external money events. An **entitlement** permits a particular benefit subject to current Access and lifecycle. An **owned item** is a separately identifiable thing whose holder can change under explicit transfer rules. A **balance** is a financial claim denominated in an asset/unit. Library holdings, copies, labels and progress are personal facts, not any of these authorities.

**Documented external distinction.** Steam packages grant application/depot licences; inventory separately marks item definitions tradable or marketable. Its market permits only eligible inventory items, settles proceeds into Steam Wallet and does not allow withdrawing those proceeds to a bank or transferring Wallet funds to another account. Trade holds delay delivery; market holds delay listing availability. A game licence, a marketable inventory instance and Wallet credit are therefore not interchangeable. [Packages](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/packages), [inventory schema](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/inventory/schema), [Market FAQ](https://help.steampowered.com/en/faqs/view/61f0-72b7-9a18-c70b), [trade/market holds](https://help.steampowered.com/en/faqs/view/34A1-EA3F-83ED-54AB), [subscriber agreement](https://store.steampowered.com/subscriber_agreement/)

**Inference:** transferability, cash withdrawal and current content access must each be deliberately specified. Neither an order nor the word “ownership” supplies all three. **Recommendation:** do not create transferable items for today's ordinary account-bound content licences merely to fill a future architecture diagram.

## One kernel, explicit module ports and native homes

All modules below are inside the existing kernel and durable runtime. A module is an authority and a versioned port, not a deployment, database service or new DBOS process. Existing writes remain one-home commands; foreign work uses named keys, owner authorization and durable effects. The following future ports/homes are **proposals**, except where explicitly described as existing. Names illustrate responsibilities, not exported signatures. The current first target/action/version/current-use Access integration and subject-discovery port remain a Root/Content/Accounts choice with Semantic participation; the [purchase contract](../services/kernel/src/commerce/README.md#indispensable-shared-choice-still-pending) owns that boundary. ## Future ledger contract

The adopted foundation is append-only immutable transactions/postings, balanced per asset/currency, with explicit account, asset, unit and exact integer amount. External and business references are idempotent; corrections are new reversals/adjustments, never edits to posted history. Payments, wallets, issuance, market, exchange and creator settlement all post through **one ledger port**. This does not mean one unbounded worldwide account partition or an additional deployment today.

**Engineering evidence.** TigerBeetle distinguishes accounting accounts/transfers and debit/credit meaning. Its linked events demonstrate an atomic group, while noting that the grouping association must be retained in business data. Its pending transfers separately reserve value and later post, void or expire; its reliable-submission guidance retains a stable identity across retries. These are relevant patterns, not a recommendation to introduce TigerBeetle or copy its API. [Financial accounting](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/financial-accounting/), [linked events](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/linked-events/), [two-phase transfers](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/two-phase-transfers/), [reliable submission](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/reliable-transaction-submission/)

Recommended conceptual transaction:

```text
transactionId, schemaVersion, bookId
businessReference, externalReferences[], semanticDigest
occurredAt, recordedAt, sourceEvidenceIds[], reversalOf?
postings[] = { accountId, assetId, unitScale, side: debit|credit, units }
```

Amounts use a lossless integer representation with an explicit scale, never binary floating-point money. Each admitted group validates debit total equals credit total **for each asset**, applies account restrictions/reservations and commits all postings in one ledger-book command. A repeated business reference with the same digest returns the original transaction; changed semantics conflict. A reversal points to the original and is itself idempotent. Provider event ID alone is insufficient as business-effect identity: different events can describe the same charge or refund. References include provider account/namespace and live/test mode.

Book ownership must be selected before enabling financial modules. A single book can contain multiple assets if every asset balances and the complete bounded posting group is local. Cross-book operations need explicit clearing/inter-book accounts, reservations, an idempotent settlement protocol and reconciliation; they do not gain a cross-partition SQL transaction. If that model is not adopted, restrict the product to a single compatible book. A ledger cannot retroactively prove complete historical events that were never retained.

Cutover must choose evidenced historical reconstruction versus reconciled opening balances at a declared date, retaining the source and unresolved differences. Opening balances cannot be presented as reconstructed transaction history. Accounting and provider evidence must determine these policies before ledger activation.

## One-home effects and financial custody

**Recommendation.** An ordinary module command authorizes the current actor, checks expected revision/constraints, writes its own aggregate, principal-scoped receipt and durable effect together, then releases its transaction. The receiver validates source authority, version, semantic digest and stable business/effect identity in its own command. It returns `applied`, `already-applied`, a semantic conflict or a retriable unavailability result. Reconciliation enumerates bounded owner-leading pending windows, retries by exact identity and compares acknowledged evidence; it never scans every buyer to rediscover an obligation. Use the existing durable runtime and effect delivery, not another shared DBOS process.

Native receipt retention, provider idempotency retention and append-only financial identity are distinct. Replay must reauthorize person-attributed actions and observe lifecycle fences. Mechanically finishing an already committed financial obligation is an owner-defined reconciliation action, not impersonating an erased account. Failed provider calls remain pending/unknown until observed; they do not create permission to repeat an irreversible external action under a fresh identity.

### Ownership and entitlement lifetime

A future item records `itemId`, edition/class, current holder, ownership generation, transfer policy and any custody/hold reference. A transfer commits once at the stable item home with an expected generation; it records both parties and immutable transfer evidence. Inventory projections can lag. Changing the holder does not transfer an account's personal Library progress or payment history.

An item-derived entitlement binds its source item and generation. Existing Access must check current ownership/custody eligibility when authorizing use; an eventual old-holder grant cannot remain sufficient after transfer. New-holder projection lag may deny temporarily, but must not authorize both holders through stale grants. Whether a listing/hold blocks only transfer or also use is a product policy; recommend a transfer-only hold until settlement, with access changing at ownership commit. Purchased nontransferable entitlements keep their own lifetime and are not converted implicitly. Gifts, licence transfer eligibility, creator resale royalties and lawful revocation after downstream resale require adoption before any transfer operation.

### Community market

The proposed market admits only explicitly marketable item classes and eligible holders. A listing captures item/generation, ask price/asset, fee policy and any trade-hold expiry. Item-home reservation prevents double listing/transfer. Canceling an unsold listing releases that reservation idempotently; it does not erase listing history. A market hold and a payment reservation have separately named deadlines and owners. The Steam precedent demonstrates that security-related listing/delivery delays are product mechanics, not just a database lock. [Hold behavior](https://help.steampowered.com/en/faqs/view/34A1-EA3F-83ED-54AB)

For a purchase, a trade aggregate records both reservations and one monotonic `commit` or `abort` decision through the existing durable runtime. Before commit, both reservations must be valid and protect custody against expiry; an abort must be acknowledged by the item/cash owners before value is released. After commit, named owner operations finish idempotently: funds can be held in escrow/clearing until item delivery is acknowledged, then seller proceeds/fees are posted. Never release a cash hold while a delayed transfer can still commit, or reverse payment automatically after the item has been irreversibly delivered. Stalled committed delivery remains an explicit reconciliation obligation; compensation after completion is a new evidenced transaction, not deletion of a trade.

All monetary legs, fees and release/reversal of reserves use the ledger port; item custody remains item-home authority. Public price history uses bounded time buckets of completed trades, with instrument/unit/currency and corrections explicitly represented; asks, canceled orders and internal seller identities are not executed prices. The module is a future capability, not a claim that Steam's private settlement algorithm works this way or that a marketplace is currently authorized.

### Full exchange

An exchange additionally needs adopted instrument eligibility, order types, price/time or other priority, precision/tick/lot rules, cancel/replace semantics, sequencing, partial fills, self-trade policy and market-data contracts. Its order book is not the Commerce offer catalogue. Each accepted order reserves the deliverable asset/funds before matching; the book home serializes accepted instructions. A fill has a stable identity, exact legs/fees and linked reservation references. Matching creates a settlement obligation, and custody/ledger acknowledgements establish completion. Replayed fills cannot settle twice; canceling the unfilled remainder cannot cancel a completed fill.

Cross-asset postings balance each asset independently; a price does not let one debit in currency A balance a credit in currency B. A bounded atomic settlement is possible only when all ledger legs share an admitted book home. Cross-book or external custody needs an adopted delivery-versus-payment protocol, clearing/reserve policy and failure recovery; no distributed atomicity is assumed. Do not activate exchange behavior until custody, jurisdiction, accounting and operational duties are decided. The current direct-sale payment setup is no evidence of exchange authorization.

## Bounded work and later extraction

**Recommendation.** Offer and entitlement operations retain the account/subject/benefit bounds in [the research report](research/commerce-ownership-entitlements-benefits.md#cost-existing-reads-versus-proposed-mechanisms). Future ledger commands admit a fixed maximum posting count and exact account keys per transaction; balance/statement reads use account-leading keysets or bounded time windows. Item operations touch one item generation and named reservations. Market reads use item/listing keys or bounded instrument/time buckets. A matching command needs an explicit maximum fills/price levels and continuation; “match the whole book” is unbounded even if its response is short. Reconciliation must cap both candidate windows and external calls. No cross-home SQL join or Library-as-authority shortcut is allowed.

Future outcome/growth axes should hold requested neighborhood fixed while increasing unrelated offers/orders/items, account postings, expired holds, receipts and revocation history. Separately grow one hot instrument's admitted orders, fill fan-out and pending settlements to expose contention/backlog. Record native rows, blocks, lock time, effect lag and external latency, with conservation/no-double-effect outcomes. These are later measurement questions, not new harness work, experiments or performance claims here.

There is no speculative service topology now. Consider extraction only after a measured failure of in-kernel indexing, admission, concurrency and isolation controls, and preserve the **same versioned port, authorization context, stable business reference/digest, evidence envelope, typed outcomes and reconciliation interface**. Suggested measurable decision gates, requiring maintainer adoption:

- **Latency/isolation:** an adopted port p99 budget is exceeded in three consecutive seven-day windows at admitted production load, with at least 10,000 observations per window; traces attribute over half of excess time to the module. Extract only if an isolated pilot meets that budget without weakening consistency.
- **Scale:** one module consumes over 50% of kernel CPU or storage I/O during peak windows on at least 14 days of a month, or requires at least four times the capacity growth of the rest; a measured partition/index/concurrency remedy cannot meet the next agreed load target.
- **Security/custody:** an external authorization or custody condition requires an independently controlled key/credential, data boundary or operator duty that kernel isolation cannot satisfy; require a written threat/control requirement and an access-boundary demonstration, not a generic “finance is sensitive” preference.
- **Operational ownership:** a separately staffed on-call owner has a distinct recovery/RTO requirement, and shared rollout/recovery has caused two attributed customer-impacting incidents in a quarter despite isolation changes. Demonstrate independent recovery and replay compatibility before splitting.

These thresholds are proposed decision criteria, not current measurements or automatic permission to deploy. A ledger consistency boundary cannot be split merely because a module crosses a CPU threshold. Any split must preserve local atomic posting scope and explicit inter-home settlement.

## Adoption boundaries

1. Keep the adopted separate identities and one fixed-version benefit/quantity-one purchase. Bind the actual first target/version/action, sale authority, current-use Access and subject discovery with their shared owners; do not infer a paid origin from Content images.
2. Apply the delivered provider-evidence findings through the sole purchase contract: immutable historical facts, complete associations and explicit unknowns. Leave balances/postings unimplemented; decide commercial/shared additions and accounting/clearing/cutover policy before activation.
3. Before transfer or market work, adopt item-home ownership generation, escrow/hold semantics, effect/reconciliation failure handling and the applicable external conditions. Before exchange work, additionally adopt matching, custody and settlement guarantees.
4. Retain one kernel/runtime and versioned ports. Any extraction needs measured evidence against an adopted gate, with the same authorization, idempotency, evidence and atomicity contract.

These decisions are compatible with today's account-bound direct sales and deferred points. They do not authorize additional financial products, new financial balances, a permission engine or a competing owner contract.
