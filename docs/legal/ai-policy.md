**1. Disclose material AI use**

Prose, artwork and translations must carry a structured AI-use declaration tied to the relevant revision:

- **None:** no generative AI materially contributed to the submitted expression.
- **Assisted:** a person substantially created the work, with material AI help in developing, rewriting, editing or producing part of it.
- **Generated:** AI produced a substantial part of the expression, including machine-generated translations, even if a person later selected or edited it.

Ordinary non-generative tools, such as formatting or basic spellchecking, do not alone require an assisted label. If a tool generates or materially rewrites expression, disclose that use.

Human review is recorded separately. Marking generated content as reviewed does not change its origin to “none.” A human-review declaration means a person actually checked the relevant revision; it is not a guarantee of accuracy or rights clearance.

**2. Keep declarations current**

Disclosures apply separately where appropriate to text, artwork and translations. Update them when a revision materially changes the contribution of AI.

Importers and automated contributors must preserve known provenance and uncertainty. Missing disclosure must not silently become “none.”

Readers may use available disclosure filters. A filter describes declared provenance, not a definitive technical determination of authorship.

**3. Permission and responsibility**

You remain responsible for submitting lawful material and accurately describing your rights. An AI label does not establish ownership, permission, originality or the absence of infringement.

Do not submit another person’s confidential work to an AI service without authority. Permission to read, host, translate, summarize or analyse a work does not automatically include permission to train a model on it.

Public content remains subject to its stated licence and applicable law. These terms do not add an AI-training licence to every public contribution.

AI output may be inaccurate, misleading, infringing or similar to someone else’s output. Check facts, citations, permissions and translations before publishing or relying on them.

**4. Private drafts and reading records**

REZICS does not train models on private drafts or reading records by default. We do not treat account registration, acceptance of general terms, or use of an AI tool as training consent.

A training opt-in must separately identify the information, recipient, purpose and available withdrawal controls. It must be optional and must not be preselected.

When you request an AI task involving private material, we process only the context authorized for that task. External processing must be disclosed before transfer, including provider retention and training settings. A provider whose terms conflict with these promises cannot be used for that private material under the ordinary feature permission.

Private reading history is not shared merely through delegation of an Agent or Realm. Any personal assistant feature using it needs a separate, specific authorization compatible with the account’s privacy controls.

**5. Automated actors**

Official and third-party automated actors must disclose that they are automated, identify the responsible operator and act within scoped permissions.

An AI system cannot become a person, claim ungranted authority, approve its own work as human-reviewed or fabricate reviews, votes and community participation.

Knowledge agents must identify sources and distinguish evidence from inference. They may build from licensed, public-domain or otherwise authorized material; access to a complete copyrighted work is not itself permission to reproduce it.

We may require approval before publication or another consequential action. Revocation applies to pending and running tasks as well as new requests.

**6. Safety and moderation**

AI cannot be used to create, solicit or distribute child exploitation, non-consensual intimate imagery, credible threats or other prohibited material.

Moderation tools may help identify spam, misclassification and abuse. We consider their limitations, including language and cultural differences. An AI detector is never the sole verdict on whether a person used AI or violated policy.

Humans decide appeals. Sensitive child-safety or intimate-image evidence must not be submitted to general-purpose generative AI systems.
