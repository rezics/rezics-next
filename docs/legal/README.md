**1. Draft status and factual placeholders**

Every `[REZICS TO FILL: ...]` marker requires a factual answer, an implemented operating choice or confirmation against the deployed service. Replace each marker with accurate text before publishing the affected policy.

The draft policies deliberately do not invent:

- Company addresses, jurisdiction, contact mailboxes or working forms.
- A registered DMCA agent, NCMEC registration or PhotoDNA approval.
- Independent appeal staff or continuous staffed monitoring.
- Processor contracts, retention periods or international-transfer safeguards.
- EU representatives or GDPR certification.
- Payment-provider approval, a merchant of record or live commerce.
- A default open licence for user works or imported catalogue data.

The repeated contact fields must resolve consistently. A privacy request, safety report or appeal must remain possible without a working account.

The most urgent factual work is to establish working public safety intake, a reachable primary and backup, deadline tracking, restricted evidence custody and a tested response to child exploitation, NCII and credible threats. A written promise is not the operating process.

**6. Legal and operational references**

The statutory removal process and deadline come from the [TAKE IT DOWN Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ12/html/PLAW-119publ12.htm) and [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-take-it-down-act). Ordinary moderation appeals must not delay that process.

Child-safety reporting and one-year preservation were checked against [18 U.S.C. §2258A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2258A). The statute does not itself impose general scanning; upload matching is a REZICS safety commitment. The [NCMEC CyberTipline](https://www.ncmec.org/gethelpnow/cybertipline) is the relevant reporting service.

DMCA requirements were checked against [17 U.S.C. §512](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap5.html#512) and the [Copyright Office’s Section 512 resources](https://www.copyright.gov/512/). The designation currently costs $6 and expires after three years unless renewed; verify the actual filing and deadline. [Copyright Office FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/faq.html).

Privacy request timing and verification were checked against [EDPB guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/respect-individuals-rights_en). Under-13 issues require more than a minimum-age clause; see the [FTC COPPA guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions).

The proposed three-function cookie limit requires a configuration check: Cloudflare can issue additional security cookies, including through [Turnstile pre-clearance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/additional-configuration/hostname-management/pre-clearance/). Do not publish an inaccurate cookie inventory.

Stripe’s [restricted-business rules](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses) and [Managed Payments documentation](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments) support the payment restrictions and conditional merchant-of-record language. Provider approval remains a fact to establish.

**7. What a lawyer should review first when affordable**

1. **Urgent safety procedures and evidence custody.** Review TAKE IT DOWN request validity, copy removal, restoration, reporting under Section 2258A, preservation, emergency disclosures, operator exposure and applicable local reporting duties. Confirm that the implementation can meet the commitments already made.
2. **Privacy and international operation.** Review GDPR and DSA territorial scope, EU representatives, processor agreements, transfers, sensitive reading information, children’s data, US state-law thresholds and privacy-request handling. The representative deferral must be assessed explicitly; the user count is not the applicable test.
3. **Market and age restrictions.** Review the US, Taiwan, Singapore, Japan, South Korea and EEA matrix, incidental UK access, contracting capacity, age assurance and any adult-content features before expansion.
4. **Copyright and contribution licences.** Review DMCA implementation, repeat-infringer decisions, translation permissions, import-source licences, catalogue and wiki licensing, moral rights and deletion against revision retention.
5. **Distribution and consumers.** Review REZICS’s reseller/distributor status, payment-provider approval, merchant-of-record allocation, the net-receipts definition, tax withholding, creator-country payouts, consumer withdrawal and refund rights, and continuing downloads after termination.
6. **General enforceability.** Review acceptance flows, governing law, liability terms, organizational authority, multilingual notice, policy amendments and any future points or purchased-credit programme.

Do not wait for paid review to operate the day-one catastrophic-harm processes. Do not describe postponed legal work as completed.
