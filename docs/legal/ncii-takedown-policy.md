**1. What this policy covers**

REZICS prohibits intimate images and videos published without the consent of the person depicted. This includes authentic material, voyeuristic imagery and intimate digital forgeries or sexual deepfakes of identifiable people.

Consent to making an image, sharing it privately or sharing it on another occasion does not by itself mean consent to its publication on REZICS.

Threatening to publish intimate material and using it for extortion are also prohibited. You may report a threat even when no image has been published.

Our platform rules may prohibit material beyond the statutory definition. Sexually explicit images are prohibited on REZICS regardless of consent.

**2. Who may request removal**

The identifiable person depicted, or an authorized person acting for them, may submit a TAKE IT DOWN removal request.

A parent, guardian, lawyer or trusted helper may act with appropriate authority. Where authority or safety is uncertain, we seek only the clarification needed to act. We do not require a victim to confront an uploader.

Anyone may report suspected NCII as a safety concern. A bystander report can trigger protective action even if it does not contain everything needed for a statutory request.

A person depicted as a minor may seek help regardless of their current age or eligibility for a REZICS account.

**3. What to include**

Send a written request containing:

1. Your physical or electronic signature, or the signature of your authorized representative. A typed name intended as a signature is acceptable.
2. Identification of the intimate depiction and information reasonably sufficient for us to locate it, such as a REZICS URL, post identifier or a description of its location.
3. A brief statement that you have a good-faith belief the identified depiction was published without your consent, with relevant information supporting that statement.
4. Information sufficient for us to contact you or your authorized representative safely, such as an email address.

If someone submits for you, they should identify that they are acting with authority on your behalf.

You may use this wording:

“I am the person depicted, or I am authorized to act for that person. I request removal of the intimate depiction located at: [location]. I have a good-faith belief it was published without the depicted person’s consent. Relevant information: [brief explanation]. Safe contact: [contact]. Electronic signature: [name].”

We do not require a perjury declaration, copyright ownership, a court filing or consent to a court’s jurisdiction for this process.

Do not re-upload or email the intimate image. We can investigate material already hosted on REZICS. We do not routinely require government ID or a selfie; if a specific verification issue arises, we seek a proportionate, safer method.

These requirements reflect [Section 3 of the TAKE IT DOWN Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ12/html/PLAW-119publ12.htm).

**4. The 48-hour process**

After receiving a valid request, we remove the reported intimate depiction as soon as possible and no later than 48 hours after receipt. Within that period, we make reasonable efforts to identify and remove known identical copies on our platform.

The 48 hours are elapsed hours, including weekends and holidays. The clock does not start when a moderator opens the request, when the uploader answers, or when an appeal finishes.

We record receipt time, provide a case reference through a safe contact route and notify you of the outcome. If essential information is missing, we promptly explain what is needed. We do not delay a valid request by demanding unnecessary information. Apparent serious harm may justify restriction before a request is complete.

These obligations are described in the [FTC’s TAKE IT DOWN guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-take-it-down-act).

**5. Copies and reappearance**

You do not need to find every duplicate.

Our response covers controlled copies and presentations, including relevant posts, media files, thumbnails, previews, accessible revisions, search results and caches. We also assess altered versions under our broader NCII prohibition, even when they are not identical copies for statutory purposes.

We use available matching and access controls to prevent identified material from reappearing. A matching tool can miss material or make errors; a negative result is not proof of consent.

We cannot directly remove copies on unrelated services or files already held outside our control. We remove REZICS-hosted material and controlled access paths and address prohibited links or previews on our service.

**6. Privacy and preservation**

Only people handling the case receive necessary information. We do not publish your request or routinely reveal your contact details to the uploader. We may disclose information when legally required or necessary for a lawful safety response.

Removal from access does not necessarily mean immediate destruction of restricted evidence. Retention follows our Privacy Policy and applicable preservation requirements. Material involving child exploitation is also handled under our Child Safety Policy.

We do not send intimate evidence to general-purpose AI tools or ask volunteer Realm moderators to circulate it.

**7. Review and other help**

You may request review of our response through [REZICS TO FILL: account-free appeal route]. Uploaders may challenge a mistaken restriction through the same route, but a challenge does not pause the removal deadline or automatically restore the content.

NCII review is separate from DMCA counter-notices. We do not require a victim to file a lawsuit to keep NCII removed.

If there is immediate danger, contact local emergency services. Additional resources include [NCMEC’s Take It Down service](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org/) and [StopNCII.org](https://stopncii.org/). These are separate services; this policy does not claim that REZICS participates in their matching networks.

If a covered platform fails to comply with the removal process, you may report it through the [FTC’s TAKE IT DOWN reporting service](https://takeitdown.ftc.gov/).
